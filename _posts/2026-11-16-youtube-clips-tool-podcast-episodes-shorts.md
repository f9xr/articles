A long podcast episode usually contains several moments that could stand alone. Finding them used to take a producer, a transcript and an afternoon. YouTube now puts that job inside Studio, next to the upload button.

On November 12, 2026, TeamYouTube expanded the YouTube clips tool in Studio to Shorts. A creator can pick a segment from a podcast episode, and YouTube turns the widescreen source into a vertical Short with captions added automatically. AI can suggest which moments to cut, if the creator is in a supported market.

The announcement is short. The practical consequences are not. The YouTube clips tool changes how a podcast becomes a discovery engine, and it draws a line between two formats that look similar but behave differently.

> **Quick answer:** The YouTube clips tool now publishes vertical Shorts from long-form podcast episodes. It reframes 16:9 video, adds editable automatic captions and offers AI-suggested moments and transcript outlines in 10 countries and seven languages. Shorts made this way get an automatic related-video link to the source episode. Standard clips do not.

## What the YouTube Clips Tool Actually Does

The workflow sits inside YouTube Studio. Open Content, choose Videos, select an existing upload and pick Clips and Shorts from the left menu. Then choose whether you want a Short or a standard video clip.

From there, you select the segment in one of two ways. You can highlight text in the transcript, or enter start and end timestamps in the timeline. Both show the selection on the video timeline, and both let you adjust the edges before you preview.

The Short then goes through a preview stage. YouTube applies automatic captions where they are available, and you can change the font, color, styling and position, or switch captions off. The final render may look better than the preview. YouTube warns that the preview can be lower resolution and may show black bars that become a blurred crop in the published version.

When you are happy, select Create Draft and finish the normal upload dialog. The result is a new upload on your channel, owned by you.

That difference matters. The viewer-facing Clips feature, which let anyone cut a segment of up to 60 seconds from any video, was retired in April 2026. The Studio tool belongs to the channel owner. It produces a new asset you control.

## The AI Part: Suggested Moments and Outlines

Two AI features sit on top of the manual workflow.

The first is a list of suggested segments. When the tool opens, it looks for passages that can be understood without the rest of the episode and offers them for review. A creator can publish a suggestion unchanged, and YouTube may offer a suggested title.

The second is an AI-generated outline of the transcript. It breaks a long episode into navigable sections and helps a creator find a specific speaker turn before cutting manually.

Neither feature is universal. [YouTube's help page for the tool](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15824265?hl=en) lists 10 countries and seven languages.

| AI suggestions and outlines | Available |
|---|---|
| Countries | Brazil, Canada, France, Germany, India, Mexico, South Korea, Spain, United Kingdom, United States |
| Languages | English, French, German, Hindi, Korean, Portuguese, Spanish |
| Manual clipping | Available globally |

Italy, Japan and Australia are not on the country list. The help page carries no date, so it does not show when each market was added. YouTube says it is working to expand access, without a timeline.

The comparison with April is worth noting. On April 17, 2026, TeamYouTube said suggested clips and outlines were limited to English-language videos in a podcast playlist in the US and Canada. Seven months later, the Shorts version is live and the AI coverage has grown from two countries and one language to 10 countries and seven languages. Creators outside those markets keep the manual tool.

## Vertical Reframing and Captions

The core technical change is framing. A podcast recorded in 16:9 has to become 9:16. YouTube says it keeps the most important parts of the video in the frame, but its documentation does not explain how it chooses them, whether the crop follows the active speaker or whether it stays fixed.

That is a detail to check on your first few Shorts. A two person interview can crop badly if the frame centers between the speakers. A solo host usually survives the reframe with less trouble.

Captions are generated from the same transcript the tool uses for selection. They are editable, and YouTube says they appear where automatic captions are available. The help page does not describe captions for standard video clips, so treat caption support as a Shorts feature for now.

One practical point: the transcript is doing double duty. It powers selection, the outline and the captions.

If your episode audio is muddy or your speakers overlap, all three get worse. Treat clean audio as an input to the workflow.

## Shorts vs Standard Clips: The Link-Back Difference

This is the part most summaries skip. The two outputs do not behave the same way.

| Feature | Short | Standard video clip |
|---|---|---|
| Shape | Vertical 9:16 | Original shape, usually 16:9 |
| Automatic captions | Yes, editable | Not described in the help page |
| Source video as related video | Added automatically | You add it to the description yourself |
| Where it lives | Shorts tab | Videos tab |
| Best for | Discovery and reach | Context and longer excerpts |

A Short made with the tool automatically lists the [source episode as a related video](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14075157?hl=en), unless the Short is private, unlisted or made for kids. That link is the funnel. A viewer who finds the clip can tap through to the full episode.

A standard clip does not get that automatic link. YouTube asks creators to add it to the description themselves. If your goal is to send new listeners back to the long episode, the Short is the format that does the work by default.

That single difference should shape your publishing plan. Do not cut a great moment, publish it as a standard clip and wonder why it sends no traffic.

## Why YouTube Built This for Podcasts

YouTube has become a large podcast platform, and the numbers explain the product decision.

[PPC Land's October 2025 coverage](https://ppc.land/youtube-podcast-setup-two-distinct-paths-for-creators/) put YouTube's podcast audience at more than 1 billion monthly active viewers. A Sounds Profitable survey published on October 1, 2026 found that 63% of US podcast consumers used YouTube for podcasts.

The clip behavior is already there. In Sounds Profitable's Podcast Atlas, presented on June 26, 2026, 71% of podcast listeners said they would follow a creator from long-form episodes to short clips. And 84% of clip consumers said clips made them regular listeners at least some of the time.

YouTube also has a living-room audience that audio platforms do not. [TechCrunch reported](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/14/youtube-viewers-watch-2-billion-hours-of-shorts-on-tvs-each-month/) in May 2026 that viewers watched 2 billion hours of Shorts on TVs each month. A podcast clip that works on a phone can also work on a television in a living room where nobody is holding the screen, which changes how large your captions and on screen text need to be.

The logic is straightforward. YouTube already hosts the long episode, and it wants the short moments to stay on the platform rather than being exported to another editor.

## The Business Case: One Recording, Several Entrances

For a business that publishes a podcast, the return is more entrances to the same content, created from a recording that has already been paid for in studio time, guest booking and editing.

A single episode can produce a handful of Shorts, each aimed at a different question a customer might ask. One clip answers a pricing question. Another explains a common mistake. A third tells a short customer story.

Each one is a door into the full conversation.

That is cheaper than producing separate short videos. The recording, the guest and the set already exist. The marginal cost is review time; the marginal return is a discovery surface that keeps working after the episode goes live.

There is a catch. A clip only helps if it makes sense on its own. A sentence that lands inside a 40 minute conversation can sound cryptic in a 60 second vertical video.

Review every suggestion with the sound off, then with the sound on. If the clip needs a paragraph of setup, it is not a clip yet.

## A Practical Workflow for Your Next Episode

You do not need a new production pipeline. Add these steps to the one you already have.

1. **Record with the vertical frame in mind.** Keep speakers closer to the centre and avoid wide two-shots where one person sits at the far edge. You will not control the crop, but you can give it better material.
2. **Publish the full episode first.** The tool works on an existing video, so the long upload is the source of truth.
3. **Open Clips and Shorts in Studio.** Choose Short, then start with a suggestion if your market has them. If not, use the transcript to find a self-contained answer.
4. **Watch the preview with captions on.** Check that the crop keeps the speaker visible and that the captions do not cover a face or a lower third.
5. **Trim the first second.** Many clips start with a breath or a filler word. A tighter opening improves retention.
6. **Write a title that states the payoff.** A question works well. So does a specific number or a named mistake. Avoid a title that only makes sense after watching.
7. **Publish as a Short, not a standard clip, when the goal is discovery.** This keeps the automatic related-video link to the full episode.
8. **Keep the podcast playlist clean.** YouTube recommends only full episodes in a podcast playlist. Shorts belong in the Shorts tab, not the podcast feed.

## Playlists, YouTube Music and the Podcast Feed

YouTube's advice is specific: keep the podcast playlist for full episodes. Clips and Shorts appear in the channel's standard Videos and Shorts tabs, so the podcast feed stays clean.

That rule has a second effect. Shorts created to support a podcast do not appear in YouTube Music. A podcast playlist should hold full-length episodes, and podcast features should not be added to separate clip playlists.

The reason is partly product logic and partly measurement. YouTube defines a podcast show as a playlist, and its episodes as the videos in that playlist. Mixing 60 second clips into that playlist would blur what a podcast episode is.

## Monetization and Originality: What Changes in 2027

Three rules sit around this feature, and they pull in different directions.

**Your own content is safe.** YouTube's help page says creators will not be penalised for using their own content in a clip or Short. That answers a question the October 1, 2026 originality update left open. That update reduced the reach of Shorts re-uploaded from other creators.

**Shorts pay has a higher bar from February 1, 2027.** Channels will need 10 million qualified Shorts views over a rolling 90 days to earn from the Shorts Creator Pool in a given month. Falling short does not affect long-form earnings. A podcast's episodes keep earning through long-form ads either way.

**The format is already large.** YouTube has spent years pushing Shorts onto television screens and into recommendations. The clip tool gives podcasters a native way to feed that surface without learning a separate editor.

One limit remains: none of this guarantees that a clip will travel. The tool removes friction. It does not remove the need for a moment worth watching.

## What the Tool Does Not Do

The announcement leaves several questions unanswered, and they matter before you plan a whole season around it.

- **It does not explain the crop.** YouTube says it keeps the important parts in frame, but not how it decides.
- **It sets no length limit for a suggested segment.** The Short itself is capped at three minutes, but the tool does not promise that every suggestion fits.
- **It does not address RSS-fed podcasts.** The reframing assumes a filmed 16:9 episode. Shows that reach YouTube through an RSS feed use static cover art, and the help page says nothing about that route.
- **It does not carry sponsor disclosures.** The post does not say whether a paid read inside an episode stays disclosed when the clip is cut. Check your own sponsorship terms.
- **It does not report clip performance separately in the announcement.** You will need to use the normal Shorts analytics and the source video's Clips and Shorts tab.

If you run a local business rather than a podcast, the same logic still applies. Short vertical answers are becoming a search surface in their own right. ## Key Takeaways

- On November 12, 2026, YouTube expanded the Studio clips tool to publish vertical Shorts from long-form podcast episodes.
- Shorts get automatic captions, 16:9 to 9:16 reframing and an automatic related-video link to the source episode.
- Standard video clips do not get that automatic link, so choose the Short format when discovery is the goal.
- AI suggestions and transcript outlines cover 10 countries and seven languages, while manual clipping works globally.
- Keep the podcast playlist for full episodes only. Clips belong in the Shorts and Videos tabs.

## Conclusion

The YouTube clips tool lives inside the upload flow, which makes it a distribution decision as much as an editing one. One recording now produces a long episode and a set of short entrances, and the link between them is automatic when you choose the Short format.

Start with one episode and three clips. Check the crop, read the captions and watch what the related-video link does. If listeners move from the clip to the full episode, you have a repeatable process. If they do not, fix the clip before you make more.
