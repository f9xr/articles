Be honest. When was the last time you finished writing a blog post and actually went back to add proper internal links? Most business owners skip it, or they add one or two links to their homepage and call it done. ## Why Internal Linking Still Matters in 2026

Internal linking used to be talked about purely in terms of Google rankings. Passing link equity, helping crawlers discover pages, keeping readers on your site longer. All of that is still true. But there is a second reason it matters now that did not exist a few years ago.

AI search tools like ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, and Claude itself learn a lot about a website by how its pages connect to each other. A cluster of well linked articles around one topic reads as a signal of real expertise. A pile of disconnected posts reads as thin content, even if each individual page is well written. If you want your site to be the kind of source an AI assistant cites, your internal linking structure is part of how it decides that. We covered the rest of that playbook in our guide on [key strategies to rank on AI search engines](https://f9xr.org/articles/2026/09/07/key-strategies-to-rank-on-ai.html).

Google's own documentation has treated [internal links as a core part of how it discovers and understands pages](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/links-crawlable){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"} for years. The signal that helps Googlebot map your site is the same one that now helps answer engines weigh your topical authority.

### What Good Internal Linking Actually Looks Like

* Links placed naturally inside the body text, not just crammed into a "related posts" box at the bottom.
* Anchor text that describes what the linked page is about, instead of generic phrases like "click here."
* A mix of links to newer content and to your older, still relevant articles, so nothing goes forgotten.
* Links that make sense for the reader first. If it would not help someone reading the sentence, it does not belong there.

## The Problem With Doing This Manually

If you run a blog with even twenty or thirty posts, keeping a mental map of what connects to what stops being realistic. You either link to the same two or three "safe" articles every time, or you skip internal linking altogether because it takes too long to search through your own archive.

This is where most small businesses and solo content creators quietly fall behind larger sites. Big publishers have tools and teams dedicated to this. You do not need a team. You need Claude and a decent prompt.

## How to Let Claude Do Interlinking for Your Next Post

Here is the actual workflow, step by step.

### Step 1: Give Claude Your Sitemap or Article List

Paste your sitemap URL, or a list of your published article titles and URLs, directly into the chat. If your sitemap is public, you can simply share the link and ask Claude to [fetch it with its web fetch tool](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/tool-use/web-fetch-tool){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"}. This gives Claude a full map of what already exists on your site before it looks at your new draft.

If your CMS does not expose a sitemap file, export your published posts as a CSV or copy the list out of the admin post screen. Anything that gives Claude the complete set of titles and URLs works, as long as it is actually complete. A partial list produces partial suggestions, and the whole point is to stop relying on your memory of what you have written.

### Step 2: Share the New Post You Just Wrote

Paste in the full draft, or upload the document if you have one. The more complete the draft, the better Claude can match specific sentences and sections to relevant older posts, rather than just guessing based on the title.

### Step 3: Ask for Contextual Suggestions, Not Just a List of URLs

This is the part people get wrong. Do not just ask "which posts should I link to." Ask Claude to point to the exact sentence or paragraph in your draft where a link would fit naturally, along with suggested anchor text. That turns a vague list into something you can copy and paste straight into your post.

A simple prompt template looks like this:

> "Here is my sitemap: [link]. Here is my new draft: [paste draft]. Read through my existing articles and suggest 4 to 6 internal links that would fit naturally inside this draft. For each suggestion, tell me the exact sentence to link from, the target URL, and suggested anchor text. Only suggest links that genuinely help the reader, not just for the sake of adding links."

### Step 4: Review Before You Publish

Claude will get most of these right, but you know your business and your content better than any AI does. Skim the suggestions, cut anything that feels forced, and add the ones that make sense. This step takes a few minutes and it is the difference between links that help your SEO and links that just clutter the page.

Also verify the target URLs exist. An AI model can occasionally suggest a page that has a sensible sounding title but was never published, so click through anything you are not sure about before the post goes live. If you want the writing around those links to pull its weight too, our guide on [how to write SEO friendly articles](https://f9xr.org/articles/2026/09/09/how-to-write-seo-friendly-articles.html) covers the paragraph level details that make both read naturally.

### Step 5: Do It Again a Month Later, in Reverse

Once a month, take one of your newer high performing posts and ask Claude to scan your older archive for pages that should now link back to it. Internal linking works both ways. New posts should link to relevant old ones, and once a new post proves itself, older posts should start pointing to it too.

If you want this wired into your publishing tools permanently instead of pasted prompt by prompt, the [Model Context Protocol](https://www.anthropic.com/news/model-context-protocol){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"} is the open standard that lets Claude connect to them directly.

## A Quick Example

Say you run a bookkeeping practice and you just published a post about "GST filing deadlines for small businesses." If your site already has older posts about "common GST mistakes" and "how to choose an accountant," a well set up Claude prompt will catch both of those and tell you exactly where in your new post to slot the links in, something like right after you mention penalties for late filing, or right where you talk about getting professional help.

The pattern holds for any business that publishes regularly. A fitness studio that just posted "five mobility drills before leg day" will watch Claude surface older pieces on recovery and warm up routines, with placement suggestions at the exact sentences where those topics come up.

Multiply that by every post you have ever written, and you can see why doing this by hand does not scale.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

* Do not accept every suggestion blindly. Claude works from what you give it, so double check that suggested URLs are actually live and correct.
* Do not overdo it. Three to six useful links per post beats ten forced ones.
* Do not skip updating your sitemap before you run this. If your sitemap is outdated, Claude cannot see your newest content.
* Do not treat this as a one time task. Revisit your linking structure every time you publish something new.
