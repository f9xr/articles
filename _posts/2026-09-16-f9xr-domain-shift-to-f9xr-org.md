## Why We Left GitHub Pages for a Custom Domain

GitHub Pages served us well in the early days. It was free, reliable, and easy to maintain while we focused on creating practical content for Indian businesses and growing startups.

Over time, the limitations became clear:

- The .github.io subdomain can look less professional to first-time visitors.
- Branding stays tied to a third-party platform.
- Custom email, advanced analytics, and certain performance tools work better on a dedicated domain.
- Search engines and AI systems tend to associate custom domains with higher authority and longevity.

Moving to f9xr.org gives us full control. It lets us present a cleaner brand, improve load times, and prepare the site for stronger visibility in Google, ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Perplexity, and other answer engines.

## How a Domain Move Supports SEO and AEO

Search engines and AI systems look for signals of stability and ownership. A custom domain is one of those signals.

When a site lives on a free platform subdomain, it can face subtle disadvantages in brand recognition and long-term authority. Moving to a dedicated domain, combined with correct redirects and consistent content, helps maintain rankings while opening the door to stronger performance.

For local businesses and startups, this same principle applies. Owning your domain makes it easier to:

- Set up professional email addresses.
- Control [Google Business Profile](https://f9xr.org/tools/local-seo-score.html) connections.
- Build consistent NAP (Name, Address, Phone) data.
- Create a clear identity that AI systems can cite with confidence.

## Practical Steps for Your Own Domain Migration

If you are considering a similar move, here is a simple checklist based on what we did:

1. **Choose a clean, memorable domain** that matches your brand.
2. **Set up 301 redirects** from every important old URL to the new equivalent. Google's [official guide on 301 redirects](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/301-redirects){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"} explains the mechanics in detail.
3. **Update Google Search Console** and resubmit your sitemap. Google's [site migration documentation](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/93633){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"} covers the steps for different migration types.
4. **Check internal links** so nothing points to the old domain.
5. **Monitor traffic and rankings** for a few weeks after the switch. Ahrefs has a practical [website migration checklist](https://ahrefs.com/blog/website-migration/){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"} that covers the monitoring side.
6. **Update social profiles, directories, and email signatures** with the new URL.

Doing these steps carefully protects the equity you have already built.
