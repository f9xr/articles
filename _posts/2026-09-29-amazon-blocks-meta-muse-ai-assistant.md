Amazon's advertising business passed $68 billion last year. Every one of those impressions is sold against a shopper who loaded a product page.

On the night of Sunday, 20 September 2026, Amazon started warning shoppers that an AI agent had been browsing its store without permission. Meta's Muse had been live for twelve days. In those twelve days it went from a product launch to the front of a fight over who gets to stand between a customer and a purchase when software does the buying.

So: Amazon blocks Meta Muse, twelve days after launch, and the argument is not really about security.

For a business that sells online, the useful question is not which side you are on. It is what happens to your order flow when access to a platform changes without warning, and what it costs to be readable by the agent standing in front of the customer instead.

## What happened when Amazon blocked Meta's Muse

Amazon blocked Meta's Muse AI agent from shopping on Amazon.com. Shoppers who sent Muse to buy something started seeing a pop-up: continued access by an unauthorized AI agent violates Amazon's Conditions of Use, to which our customers have agreed.

Amazon spokesperson Lara Hendrickson told [GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/amazon-blocks-metas-muse-ai-assistant-in-new-standoff-over-agentic-shopping/){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"} that Meta never asked to participate. She said third-party applications making purchases for customers of other businesses should operate openly and respect a service provider's decision on whether to take part, and she compared it to food delivery apps and online travel agencies, which coordinate with the merchants they serve.

Amazon also asked Meta to remove Amazon from the Muse experience. Meta declined.

Amazon's stated objections are specific. Meta did not give notice. Muse does not identify itself as an automated agent while browsing, so an undisclosed third party is moving through customer accounts. And Muse appears to capture and store customer login credentials, with access to order history and account pages when a user asks for it.

Meta disputes the security framing. The company says Muse has no visibility into passwords or payment methods, that credentials go into secure storage the agent can use without reading, and that the agent runs inside a dedicated virtual machine with approval prompts before sensitive actions. So far the two sides are describing different systems rather than the same one.

## What Meta's Muse is, and where the money sits

Muse is an agent, not a chatbot. The difference matters commercially. A chatbot returns an answer and the customer still does the buying. An agent browses, compares, fills the cart and pays.

Meta launched it on 8 September 2026, and according to [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/23/metas-standoff-with-amazon-over-muse-comes-ahead-of-meta-connect.html){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"} it rocketed to the top of Apple's App Store. At [Meta Connect 2026](https://www.meta.com/blog/meta-connect-2026-everything-we-announced/){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"} on 23 and 24 September, Zuckerberg announced Shopify catalogue access, new retail connectors including Walmart, Best Buy, Gap, Sephora, Ulta and Wayfair, Shop Pay and PayPal as payment rails, and Expedia and Instacart for travel and groceries.

The revenue argument is where the money sits. Morgan Stanley has estimated Muse could produce roughly $1.3 billion in annual revenue by 2028, based on transaction volume rather than advertising. Raymond James has floated a far larger long-run number, and JPMorgan has argued Muse could become the most widely used consumer AI application since ChatGPT. Treat all three as analyst estimates, not reported results. What they share is a direction: Meta is building a transaction business, and that puts it against the transaction and advertising businesses Amazon already runs.

## Why Amazon blocked Muse, past the official reasons

Amazon's public argument is about consent, transparency and security. Underneath sits the $68 billion advertising line, which Forbes reported as Amazon's ad revenue for last year.

Sponsored listings and personalised recommendations earn against a shopper loading a product page. An agent that queries a structured catalogue and completes the purchase elsewhere can skip the page entirely. No page view, no impression, no auction. [Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/commerce/amazon-locks-out-metas-muse-in-agentic-shopping-standoff/){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"} reports that Amazon told it agents bypass the personalisation built into its shopping experience, so customers may see products that do not reflect their history.

Amazon has its own agent. Rufus answers shopping questions inside Amazon. Buy for Me goes further and buys from retailers outside Amazon. Letting a rival's agent sit in the customer relationship weakens both, which is why the internal objection is about control and not only privacy.

This is a year-long fight, not a single incident. Amazon sued Perplexity over its Comet browser, and on 4 August 2026 the Ninth Circuit vacated the preliminary injunction, finding Amazon unlikely to succeed on the record before it in showing that Perplexity accessed Amazon's computers in violation of the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act. The federal anti-hacking route weakened, so the contractual route matters more. A federal computer-access statute was never a clean fit for a person using a tool to act through their own account. Your terms of service is the gate that held.

The hypocrisy charge also lands. Amazon blocks unidentified agents on its own storefront while its Buy for Me feature shops on third-party sites, though Amazon says that feature identifies itself as AI and lets merchants opt in. Meta restricts rival assistants inside WhatsApp under its own business terms. Each company wants open access to everyone else's customer relationship and a closed door on its own.

## What this means for small and local businesses

You are not Amazon. You do not have a legal team to write an agent policy, and you do not have 300 million daily visitors to lose. But the same forces arrive from both directions.

**The upside is a new customer type.** If an assistant can compare a dozen providers and book one, then a bakery, a dental clinic or a laptop repair shop becomes reachable by people who never open a browser tab. Those orders show up without a keyword ranking and without a click. The requirement is simple and unglamorous: your prices, hours, services and location need to be readable without a human interpreting your site design.

**The risk is concentrated dependency.** The overhead of running a business through one marketplace is low until the platform rewrites its rules. Amazon switched Muse off in twelve days with no negotiation period. If most of your orders arrive through a channel you do not control, the downside is not a slow month. You get an overnight rule change you cannot appeal.

| Question | Large marketplaces | Small and local businesses |
|---|---|---|
| Can they block agents? | Yes, and they already do | Yes, through terms, robots rules and site settings |
| Who holds the customer data? | The platform | You, if you sell from your own site |
| Who benefits from agent shopping? | The platform first, and it is the one defending against it | Businesses that are easy to read and transact with |
| Main risk | Ad revenue and control of the customer | Disappearing from AI answers without noticing |

Consumer appetite is real and so is consumer caution. Recurly's analysis of 76 million subscribers found around 43% comfortable with AI handling parts of their subscription experience, led by content personalisation. Two days after the block, [NatWest, Bank of America, ING, ASB, Capital One and Commonwealth Bank of Australia](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/22/banks-warn-ai-shopping-bots-raise-scam-fraud-data-privacy-risks.html){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"} published joint principles warning that agents raise scam, fraud and privacy risk, and that consumers are unclear who they would approach if an agent bought the wrong thing. Plan for both.

## How to prepare your business for AI shopping agents

None of this needs new budget. Most of it also improves your Google rankings, which is why it is cheap to justify.

**1. Own the storefront and the customer record.** Treat marketplaces as a channel, not a foundation. You want a fast, mobile-first site you control, and an email list that reaches your customers no matter whose rules changed this week. Our [mobile-first explainer](https://f9xr.org/articles/2026/08/03/why-go-mobile-first.html) covers the speed side. A rebuild runs in weeks rather than quarters, so the cost of getting it wrong compounds for as long as you stay live.

**2. Make your data machine-readable.** Assistants pull from structured, consistent information, not from how your page looks. Add LocalBusiness, Product, Offer, Review and FAQ schema so prices, hours, stock and services can be extracted rather than inferred. Our [schema markup guide](https://f9xr.org/articles/2026/09/26/schema-markup-generator-guide-why-it-matters.html) shows the specific types. Keep your name, address, phone, hours and service descriptions identical across your site, your Google Business Profile and every directory, character for character. [Structuring local business data for Meta's AI indexers](https://f9xr.org/articles/2026/08/19/structuring-local-business-data-meta-ai-indexers.html) goes deeper on that last point.

**3. Write an agent policy on purpose.** Amazon's block is a reminder that your terms of service now decide things. Answer three questions: do you allow agents to browse and buy, are account and checkout pages off limits to automated tools, and does your terms document mention automated agents at all? Crawlers, assistants and shopping agents are three different visitors with three different effects on your costs, so treat them differently. Our [guide to blocking AI crawlers](https://f9xr.org/articles/2026/09/04/block-ai-crawlers-on-your-site.html) covers the controls, and our [look at llms.txt and ai.txt](https://f9xr.org/articles/2026/09/08/llms-txt-ai-txt-do-they-work-2026.html) separates what works from what is still marketing.

**4. Keep checkout boring.** Agents and people both fail on confusing checkouts. Clear prices, visible shipping, stated returns, secure payment, no surprise fee at the last step. Trust signals such as real reviews and a genuine contact page help a person and a machine reach the same conclusion. Our [website trust score guide](https://f9xr.org/articles/2026/08/15/how-to-increase-website-trust-score.html) covers what to fix first.

**5. Keep your local profile current.** A complete Google Business Profile with fresh, genuine reviews is the cheapest way to be found by an assistant asked for a recommendation near you. [How AI picks local businesses in 2026](https://f9xr.org/articles/2026/08/16/how-ai-picks-local-businesses-2026.html) breaks down the selection logic.

**6. Protect customer accounts.** If agents are part of how people buy from you, tell customers how to be safe. Multi-factor authentication, periodic reviews of connected accounts, and alerts on unfamiliar ordering patterns. Never ask for a password over chat or email, and say so plainly on your site.

**7. Audit your AI visibility monthly.** Ask ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude and Perplexity the questions a customer would ask, such as who repairs laptops in your area. Record whether you appear, what is said, and whether the details are right. Then fix what is wrong. This is about twenty minutes a month, and it is the only way to catch a factual error before a customer does. [Monitoring AI search trends](https://f9xr.org/articles/2026/08/26/monitor-ai-search-trends-boost-visibility-citations.html) shows the method.

## What happens next

Amazon says it is in direct conversation with Meta and has not said whether it will sue. Four outcomes are plausible. A negotiated deal where Muse identifies itself and Amazon sets access terms. A longer standoff where Muse grows everywhere except Amazon. A legal fight on the Amazon v Perplexity model. Or industry rules on agent identity and liability, pushed by the bank group that published principles days after the block.

The bank principles matter more than they look. A group including NatWest, Bank of America, ING, Capital One and Commonwealth Bank of Australia setting common positions on agentic commerce is the first sign that payment networks may get a vote on how agents buy things.

For now, buyers who want something from Amazon should buy it on Amazon.

## Key takeaways

- Amazon began blocking Meta's Muse on Amazon.com on 20 September 2026, twelve days after Muse launched on 8 September.
- Amazon's stated grounds are notice, self-identification and credential handling. Meta disputes the security claims, saying credentials stay in storage the agent cannot read.
- Advertising is the commercial stake. Amazon's ad line topped $68 billion last year, and an agent that skips the product page removes impressions.
- The legal route shifted to contracts after the Ninth Circuit vacated Amazon's Perplexity injunction on 4 August 2026, which makes your own terms of service decisive.
- Agent shopping is growing elsewhere. Shopify's catalogue, Shop Pay, PayPal, Walmart, Best Buy, Sephora and others are connected, and Morgan Stanley models about $1.3 billion in annual Muse revenue by 2028.
- For small businesses the work is unglamorous and cheap: own the site, add schema, keep listings identical, set an agent policy, and check AI visibility once a month.

## Conclusion

Amazon and Meta are arguing about control of the customer relationship, and both have a defensible case. You do not need to pick a side. You need to reduce how much of your business depends on a platform that can change its terms in a weekend.

The measures here are hours, not lakhs of rupees. A structured data pass, a consistency sweep across your listings, a paragraph in your terms of service, and twenty minutes a month checking what AI assistants say about you. Spend the same rupees any way you like; the difference is only in what each option returns and when.
