## What Is Google's New Weather AI (WeatherNext 3)?

WeatherNext 3 is Google's flagship AI weather forecasting model, built by teams at Google DeepMind and Google Research. It's designed to replace the traditional physics-based simulations that have powered weather forecasting for decades with a deep learning model trained directly on real-world observational data.

According to Google, the model now feeds into weather information across:

* **Google Search**, the weather snippets you see when you search "weather today"
* **Google Maps**, localized conditions layered into navigation and planning
* **Gemini**, conversational weather answers inside Google's AI assistant
* **Google Maps Platform**, for developers building weather-aware apps
* **Google Cloud**, for researchers and businesses needing raw forecast data

Samier Merchant, a senior staff engineer at Google, described this as a milestone moment, noting it's the first time core weather variables generated by this kind of model will directly power so many Google products simultaneously. The [official announcement](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/introducing-weathernext-3/) fills in the technical details.

### How WeatherNext 3 Is Different From Older Forecasting Models

Traditional forecasting relies on physics-based simulations that model the atmosphere using complex equations, updated periodically using data collected from weather stations and satellites. WeatherNext 3 takes a fundamentally different approach.

Instead of relying only on periodic data updates, the model trains directly on:

* Live geostationary satellite mosaics, refreshed roughly every hour
* Surface observations from weather stations, regional mesonet networks, and ocean buoy records
* High-resolution precipitation data, including satellite-based rainfall products

Because satellite imagery now arrives within about an hour instead of the traditional six-hour gap, the model can refresh its forecasts hourly instead of every six hours, a meaningful jump for anything involving fast-changing conditions like storms or sudden rainfall.

## The Numbers Behind the Upgrade

Google has shared some specific performance claims for WeatherNext 3, and they're worth breaking down individually rather than taking as one blanket "it's better now" statement.

| Metric | Improvement | What It Means |
|---|---|---|
| Forecast resolution | Down to 5 km for temperature and moisture | Weather can now be predicted differently for areas just a few kilometers apart |
| Update frequency | Every hour, up from every 6 hours | Forecasts reflect real-time conditions much faster |
| Precipitation accuracy | Up to 50% more accurate a day or more ahead | Better rain and storm predictions for planning purposes |
| Forecast horizon | 15-day outlook, 64 ensemble scenarios | More reliable longer-range planning with built-in uncertainty ranges |
| Model size | 2.4x more parameters than WeatherNext 2 | Greater capacity to capture complex atmospheric patterns |

Google also reports that WeatherNext 3 outperformed rival AI weather systems built by Microsoft, Nvidia, and the European Center for Medium-Range Weather Forecasting when tested on Operational WeatherBench, an independent benchmarking tool built by the startup Brightband. It reportedly beat traditional government forecasting systems as well, and [TechCrunch's coverage](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/03/googles-latest-ai-weather-model-gives-you-no-excuse-to-forget-your-umbrella/) walks through the rollout.

One caveat to keep in mind: terms like "hourly," "5 km," and "15 days" describe different capabilities of the model rather than one single forecast product available instantly at the finest resolution everywhere. The 5 km resolution applies to specific surface variables like temperature and moisture, while wind speed forecasts currently run at a coarser 25 km resolution.

It's easy to assume weather AI is only relevant to meteorologists or farmers, but the ripple effects reach much further into everyday business operations.
