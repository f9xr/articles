## Did the Core Update Hit Your Site? How to Check

Do this slowly and with data, not with a gut feeling.

### Step 1: Wait Before You Judge

Google's own guidance suggests waiting until at least a full week after the rollout finishes, then comparing that week against a baseline week from before the update. If the rollout ends around November 24, a clean comparison period starts around December 1. A sensible baseline is November 3 to 9.

Making big changes in the middle of a rollout muddies your data. You will not know what helped and what hurt.

### Step 2: Compare in Search Console

Open the Performance report and compare clicks, impressions, average position and CTR for the two periods. Then break it down:

- **By page:** Which URLs lost the most?
- **By query:** Did branded searches hold while generic searches fell?
- **By device and country:** Is the drop everywhere or only in one place?
- **By search appearance:** Did anything change in Discover or other features?

## How to Recover From a Core Update: A Practical Plan

Be realistic first. Google has said improvements after a decline can take days or months and are not guaranteed. There is no switch to flip. The goal is to make your site genuinely better than the pages ranking above you.

### 1. Audit Your Content Like an Outsider

For each page that lost traffic, ask:

- Does it answer the search query fully and quickly?
- Does it say something the top ten results do not?
- Is it current, with correct prices, dates, addresses and facts?
- Would you trust it if you were spending your own money?

Use our [monthly website audit checklist](/2026/09/03/monthly-website-audit-checklist.html) as your working template.

### 2. Improve, Merge or Remove Carefully

- **Improve** pages that have potential but feel shallow. Add original photos, real examples, pricing, process steps and expert input.
- **Merge** overlapping pages that compete with each other into one stronger page, with proper redirects.
- **Remove** only as a last resort, and only for content that truly cannot be saved. Google's own documentation treats deletion as a final step.

If you have a lot of old posts, our guide on [reviving dead content with systematic audits](/2026/08/20/reviving-dead-content-systematic-audits.html) shows a process that works.

### 3. Strengthen Trust and Experience Signals

Core updates reward sites that look and act trustworthy. Make sure you have:

- Real author names with short bios and credentials
- A clear About page, contact details and physical address for local businesses
- An editorial policy and a way to report errors (see our [editorial policy page guide](/2026/10/05/editorial-policy-page-eeat-guide.html))
- Original photos and first hand experience instead of stock images
- Honest reviews and testimonials

## What Local Businesses and Small Shops Should Do Right Now

Core updates mainly reshuffle web results, but your website quality also feeds into how you are seen overall. If you run a local business:

- **Protect your peak season.** Do not redesign or migrate your site during the update. Keep Black Friday, wedding season and festive pages stable.
- **Keep Google Business Profile fresh.** Update holiday hours, offers, photos and posts. Read our guide on [ranking number 1 on Google Maps](/2026/08/16/rank-number-1-google-maps-2026.html).
