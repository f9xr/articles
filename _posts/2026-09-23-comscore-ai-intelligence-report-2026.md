Here's the number that matters most from Comscore's latest data: mobile visits to AI assistants were up **107% year over year** in June 2026. Desktop managed 18% growth over the same window. The gap is the story, and it changes how you should think about where your customers are when they ask an AI a question.

Comscore released its [Q2 2026 AI Intelligence Report](https://www.comscore.com/Insights/Press-Releases/2026/9/Comscores-Q2-2026-AI-Intelligence-Report){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"} on September 22, 2026. It is a panel-based measurement of how US consumers actually use ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Microsoft Copilot and Perplexity. The headline findings: overall adoption keeps climbing, ChatGPT's share is slipping, Gemini and Claude are taking the space, and mobile is now where the growth is.

This report is useful for a specific reason. It gives you specific numbers that tell you where AI discovery is going, and most of them you can act on without touching a dashboard. Here is what the data says, what each metric actually means, and how to read the same signals in your own analytics.

## What Each Metric in the Report Means

Before the numbers, three definitions, because the report uses these terms loosely and precision matters here.

**Visitation** counts unique people who visited an AI assistant in a given month, across desktop and mobile. It is a reach number. If the same person opens ChatGPT on her phone three times, she counts once.

**Desktop conversations** count completed, multi-step sessions on desktop. Comscore's earlier [Q1 2026 report](https://www.comscore.com/Insights/Press-Releases/2026/6/Comscore-s-Q1-2026-AI-Intelligence-Report){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"} noted users averaged 4.9 prompts per conversation on ChatGPT and 7.1 on Copilot. Seats multiplied by prompts is closer to real workload than raw visits.

**Prompt volume share** is the share of all prompts typed into major AI assistants in a period, apportioned to each tool. ChatGPT at 50% share means half of all measured prompts in June went to ChatGPT. This is the closest thing the report has to a market share metric.

## Mobile Overtook Desktop in AI Assistant Usage Growth

The +107% mobile figure is the headline, but it sits inside a broader reach trend. In June 2026, 29% of mobile users visited an AI assistant, and 35% of desktop users did. In January 2025 those numbers were 12% and 25%. Mobile nearly caught desktop in raw reach while growing far faster.

The per-tool splits make the pattern concrete. According to the report, year-over-year desktop and mobile visitation growth for the majors was roughly: ChatGPT +84% and +83%, Gemini +137% and +648%, Copilot +246% and -28%, Perplexity +265% and +516%, and Claude +297% with mobile lagging desktop for the first half of the year.

Read those two pairs together. Gemini's mobile visits grew six and a half times. Perplexity's mobile visits grew more than six times. Copilot grew on desktop but actually shrank on mobile. The one consistent theme is that no single platform dominates both ends of the device split, and the tools winning desktop share are not automatically winning mobile.

**How to read this in your own data:** in Google Analytics, segment your AI-referral traffic by device over the last 12 months. If your sessions from AI assistants are still 80% desktop, you're guessing behind the market. The growth is happening on phones, so you have to be built for the mobile reading experience, fast pages, clear answer blocks, no ten-second hero carousels.

## ChatGPT Still Leads, but Its Share Is Falling

ChatGPT reached 168 million desktop conversations in June 2026 and grew multiplatform visitation to 99 million, up from 59 million a year earlier. It is not shrinking. But its share of total AI prompt volume fell from 70% in January 2026 to 50% in June.

That is a 20-point drop in six months. It is not a ChatGPT problem, it's a category problem: more people adopted more tools, so the leader's slice of a bigger pie got thinner. The same dynamic plays out in search. When one channel controlled 70% of queries you could optimize for that channel alone. At 50% and falling, you can't.

Gemini is the biggest beneficiary. Its share of prompt volume nearly doubled from 17% to 30% in the same window, and its desktop conversations grew from 8 million to 89 million over the past year. Part of that is distribution, Gemini is bundled into Google accounts, Android and Workspace, so it converts existing Google users without any new habit. Part of it is genuine usage acceleration.

**How to read this in your own data:** list the AI platforms that actually send you referral traffic, not the ones you assume do. If Gemini or Copilot are absent from that list but your industry is B2B, you're probably leaving share on the table, because those are the tools your buyers are opening by default.

## Claude: The Breakout the Report Keeps Flagging

Claude's numbers are the report's most repeated finding, and they deserve it. US desktop conversations grew from roughly 1.1 million in October 2025 to a peak near 40 million in April 2026, a rise of about 1,858% at its strongest point. June settled at 22.3 million, still about 20 times October's level even after the summer slowdown.

Claude's share of prompt volume rose from 2% to 11% between January and June 2026. That is a more than fivefold gain in a category where the leader only holds 50%. Anthropic did it without bundling, without an operating system default, and mostly on desktop, which is why the growth reads as durable rather than promotional.

For content strategy, Claude's rise matters because Claude's citation behavior differs from ChatGPT's. Claude answers are more likely to name-check and quote sources directly, which makes visibility in Claude more measurable. If you want a sense of how that works in practice, our guide on [key strategies to rank on AI](https://f9xr.org/articles/2026/09/07/key-strategies-to-rank-on-ai.html) covers the mechanics, including how assistant citations surface to users.

**How to read this in your own data:** if your content gets cited by ChatGPT but you see little from Claude and Gemini, check whether your pages carry clean, quotable answer blocks with named sources, dates and stats. Those are the exact structures Claude favors in its replies.

## Where AI Ad Money Is Starting to Flow

The Q2 report also tracks the earliest stage of ad monetization, and one figure jumps out. In ChatGPT, ad share on hotel travel prompts rose from 6% in March 2026 to 14% in April and 24% in May. If you are in travel, hospitality or any category where intent is seasonal, that is a fast-moving target.

This is the same progression we documented when [ChatGPT Ads launched](https://f9xr.org/articles/2026/08/22/chatgpt-ads-guide-business-owners.html): ads start in high-intent categories, appear as labeled sponsored placements below the AI's answer, and creep up in share as advertisers run tests. The hotel-travel numbers are the first public evidence of how quickly that share can move.

OpenAI also confirms the answer stays independent of the ads, which means the way to win is to appear in the organic answer, not just to buy the placement. That is why [conversational ad strategy](https://f9xr.org/articles/2026/09/13/kfc-mamaearth-first-indian-brands-chatgpt-ads.html) now has two lanes: earn the citation and fund the sponsored slot.

**How to read this in your own data:** track your ad-focused keywords against AI assistant mentions, not just search clicks. A rise in "ChatGPT says X brand" style queries is an early signal that assistant awareness is converting into purchase consideration. Because these queries rarely show up in traditional keyword reports, set a search alert and a quarterly manual check.

## What the AI Reach Numbers Mean for Your Site

The reach stat is the one that should change your planning assumptions. If 35% of desktop users and 29% of mobile users visited an AI assistant in June 2026, then AI discovery is no longer experimental. [Digiday's analysis](https://digiday.com/media/comscore-data-shows-how-ai-discovery-is-splintering-beyond-chatgpt/){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"} of the same data makes the point bluntly: AI discovery is splintering beyond ChatGPT, and the tools winning differ by audience. It is a mainstream channel with the same order of magnitude as social and search referrals.

That changes the internal question from "should we be visible in AI?" to "which AI tools matter for our customers, and how do we earn a citation in each?" The answer starts with how AI models [select and cite the sources](https://f9xr.org/articles/2026/08/16/how-ai-picks-local-businesses-2026.html) they answer from. Predictable site structure, up-to-date entity data, and content that answers the question directly in the first paragraph all raise your odds of being the cited source.

None of this requires replacing your SEO program. It requires adding an AI measurement layer on top of it. ## How to Track the Same Signals in Your Own Data

The report gives you the national picture. Here is a minimal pipeline to get the per-business version, in four steps.

1. **Segment AI referrals.** In GA4, build a segment for sessions sourced from ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Copilot, Perplexity and other known AI domains. Compare this month against the same month last year, by device.
2. **Track prompt share proxies.** You cannot see ChatGPT's prompt share, but you can track your share of AI-sourced sessions and compare it to total organic. When AI share grows while organic stays flat, discovery is shifting, not shrinking.
3. **Log your citations.** Monthly, search your brand plus your core keywords in ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude and Perplexity. Record which tool cites you and from which article. Keep four rows of history; the trend is the signal.
4. **Watch mobile conversion.** Because mobile AI visits are growing so fast, check your mobile page speed and mobile conversion rate on AI referral sessions specifically. Slow phone pages will quietly cap your upside.

## Key Takeaways

- Mobile AI assistant visits grew 107% year over year in June 2026, versus 18% on desktop. The growth is on phones.
- ChatGPT still leads, but its share of prompt volume fell from 70% to 50% between January and June 2026.
- Gemini nearly doubled its prompt share to 30%, helped by bundling into Google accounts, Android and Workspace.
- Claude's desktop conversations grew about 1,858% from October 2025 to its April 2026 peak, then settled around 20 times October's level in June.
- ChatGPT ad share on hotel travel prompts climbed from 6% to 24% between March and May 2026, an early signal of AI ad monetization.
- Your move is to segment AI referrals by device, log citations monthly, and earn the organic answer before buying the sponsored placement.

Comscore's report is one of the few public datasets that tells you exactly how consumers split their AI usage across tools and devices. The headline changes are real: mobile is where growth happens, ChatGPT's monopoly on attention is over, and Gemini and Claude are splitting the remaining share. The practical translation is that AI visibility now needs a multi-platform strategy, not a single-ChatGPT checklist.
