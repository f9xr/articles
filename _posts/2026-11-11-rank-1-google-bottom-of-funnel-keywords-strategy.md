Most business owners ask the same question when they start blogging: how do I get more traffic? We build websites and run marketing for startups and local businesses, and we think that is the wrong question.

Traffic is not the goal. Customers are. A page that brings 10,000 curious visitors who never buy is worth less than a page that brings 100 people ready to hire you, book a call, or place an order.

So instead of fighting for broad, crowded keywords that giants already own, aim to rank #1 on Google for specific, high-intent, bottom-of-funnel searches you can realistically win. Think of searches like "best CRM for small teams," "Tool A vs Tool B," or "cheap website redesign for clinics." The people typing these are close to a decision, and that is exactly who you want on your page.

This guide breaks down the full strategy in plain language, with practical steps you can use on your own website this week.

> **Quick answer:** To rank #1 on Google as a smaller site, target bottom-of-funnel keywords (best, top, vs, review, cheap, alternatives) instead of broad topics. Build honest comparison pages with a methodology section and original data, publish unique versions on LinkedIn and YouTube with canonical links back to your site, and match each call to action to the reader's intent. Buyers beat traffic.

## What Is Bottom-of-Funnel SEO?

A marketing funnel has three broad stages. At the top, people are learning ("what is local SEO"). In the middle, they are exploring solutions ("how to improve local rankings"). At the bottom, they are choosing ("best local SEO company for restaurants").

Bottom-of-funnel (BOFU) SEO means creating content for that last stage. The search volume is usually lower, but the visitors are far more valuable, and the competition is often easier to beat because fewer sites bother to write for it well.

### Why chasing big keywords often fails

Broad keywords are dominated by large brands, publishers, and sites with years of authority. A new or small website can spend months writing for them and still land on page five. Even when you do rank #1 on Google for a broad term, the visitors may only be looking for a definition, not a service provider.

Specific, high-intent searches give you a fairer fight, and the readers have a reason to act.

## Commercial Keywords Explained

In SEO, keywords containing words like "best" and "top" are classified as commercial keywords. The person searching is researching and wants to compare products, services, or brands before making a final purchase decision.

| Modifier | Example search | What the searcher wants | Best content format |
|---|---|---|---|
| best | best accounting software for startups | A shortlist they can trust | Listicle |
| top | top web design agencies in Hyderabad | Leading providers to compare | Listicle with criteria |
| review | Tool X review | Honest pros and cons | Product review |
| vs | Tool X vs Tool Y | A direct head-to-head answer | Comparison post |
| cheap | cheap website for small business | Affordable options without junk | Pricing guide |
| comparison | hosting plans comparison | Side-by-side features and prices | Comparison table page |
| alternatives | Tool X alternatives | Other options after doubting one | Alternatives roundup |

If you write "best of" lists, our guide on [writing listicles without spam](https://f9xr.org/articles/2026/11/07/write-listicle-articles-without-spam-guide.html) shows how to keep them honest: state your criteria, include fair alternatives, and disclose your ties.

### A warning before you write comparison content

Do not write comparison articles if your product is not as good as the alternative, or if the competitor is stronger than you. A comparison page works because readers trust it to be fair and helpful. If you cannot come out ahead on the criteria your reader cares about, you are sending ready-to-buy visitors to someone else.

If you are the underdog, pick a smaller angle where you have a real edge, such as price, support, speed, or a specific niche, and write for that instead.

## Five Steps to Rank #1 on Google with Buyer Keywords

### 1. Pick keywords you can realistically win

Use a keyword research tool like [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"} or Semrush to find long-tail phrases that combine low keyword difficulty with strong buying intent. Look for these patterns:

- Comparisons: "X vs Y"
- Alternatives: "alternatives to X"
- Best-of lists: "best [product] for [specific audience]"
- Reviews and pricing: "X review," "cheap [service] for [audience]"

One practical tip for local businesses: add your audience or location to the phrase. "Best website builder" is a battlefield. "Best website builder for local clinics" is a conversation you can win. If the map pack is your main battleground, our [Google Maps ranking playbook](https://f9xr.org/articles/2026/08/16/rank-number-1-google-maps-2026.html) covers that side of local search.

For a deeper look at matching keywords to what people want, read our guide on [decoding search intent](https://f9xr.org/articles/2026/08/21/decoding-search-intent-what-customers-want.html).

### 2. Distribute on multiple platforms

If you do not have a website yet, or your domain authority is still weak, publishing only on your own site can be slow. Increase your ranking surface by publishing the same idea on several platforms at the same time:

- Your own website (always the home base)
- LinkedIn, using Pulse articles
- YouTube, as a video version of the article with an SEO optimized description. You can also embed the same video in your article.

Other platforms such as Medium and dev.to can also work, depending on your content category. In our view, YouTube is currently one of the best platforms for ranking user-shared content, followed by LinkedIn.

**Keep each version unique.** Do not copy and paste the same text everywhere. Rewrite the intro, change the examples, and adjust the format for the platform.

**Use canonical URLs.** Where the platform lets you set one (Medium and dev.to both support it), point the canonical to the article on your own website, as [Google's documentation on consolidating duplicate URLs](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/consolidate-duplicate-urls){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"} recommends. Where it does not, link back to the original article near the top of the post. This tells Google which version is the source.

For a wider view of this approach, see [search everywhere optimization](https://f9xr.org/articles/2026/08/14/search-everywhere-optimization-youtube-google-seo.html).

### 3. Build pages that outperform competitors

Open the top results for your target keyword and ask what a better page would include. These elements matter:

- Key takeaways near the top for quick scanning
- Comparison tables so readers see differences at a glance
- Clear author profiles with real names, experience, and links, to build authority
- Detailed FAQ sections that answer follow-up questions in short, direct language, marked up with [FAQPage](https://schema.org/FAQPage){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"} schema
- YouTube embeds where a video explains something better than text
- A title that matches the content and answers exactly what the reader came to learn
- Internal and external links that help the reader, with no link spam

Google's [guidance on helpful, people-first content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"} rewards first-hand experience, so use your own numbers from your own work wherever you can. If you use an AI tool to help write, tell it not to invent statistics, and verify every claim before you publish.

### 4. Add a methodology section

When you compare products or services, explain how you compared them, then apply the same method to every option from start to finish. A methodology section makes your article more believable and keeps you honest.

Here is a simple template you can adapt:

| Criteria | What we check | How we score |
|---|---|---|
| Pricing | Plans, hidden fees, renewal cost | Clear price, no surprises |
| Ease of use | Setup time, learning curve | Time to first result |
| Features | Core features your audience needs | Checklist match |
| Support | Channels, response quality | Tested with real questions |
| Proof | Reviews, case studies, third-party mentions | Verifiable sources only |

The table above is a sample framework, not a set of results. Replace it with the criteria your readers actually care about, and score every option the same way.

When you publish comparisons, back your claims with social proof, transparent feature lists, and third-party reviews.

### 5. Chase information gain

Information gain is the most important differentiator in this strategy. It means giving readers something they cannot find on the other ten pages that rank for the same keyword.

Here are practical ways to create it:

- Run a small survey of your own customers and publish the results.
- Share screenshots and numbers from your own tests.
- Document a real project, including what went wrong.
- Publish a pricing or turnaround benchmark from your own work.

Unique, proprietary data gives AI search engines such as ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, and Perplexity a reason to cite you, because they cannot get that insight anywhere else. If you want a framework for creating this kind of proof, our post on [competitor analysis that actually wins customers](https://f9xr.org/articles/2026/10/26/competitor-analysis-that-actually-wins-you-customers.html) is a good next read.

## Conversion Focus: CTA Congruency

This strategy only works if visitors take action. We call the answer "CTA congruency," which means your call to action must match what the reader is looking for at that moment.

| Reader intent | Matching CTA |
|---|---|
| Comparing providers | Book a free consultation call |
| Reading a checklist or guide | Download the checklist (lead magnet) |
| Reading a review | Start a free trial or request a demo |
| Reading a pricing guide | Get a custom quote |

A few rules to keep your CTAs useful:

- Place more than one CTA, but spread them naturally: one near the top, one mid-article, one at the end.
- Make sure the CTA connects the article topic to your offer.
- Do not stuff your page with random buttons. Irrelevant CTAs feel like spam and push readers away.

## Measure, Learn, Repeat

Ranking is a repeated game. Think of it as getting more shots on net. Each article is one shot. Some will miss, some will hit, and you will only know which by tracking results.

Use [Google Search Console](https://search.google.com/search-console){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"} to monitor:

- Which queries your pages appear for
- Impressions, clicks, and average position
- Pages that rank on page two and could be pushed up with a better title, table, or FAQ

Then update, republish, and publish again. Consistency beats one perfect post.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Writing "best" lists that put your own brand first without proof
- Copying the same article to every platform
- Using invented or unverified statistics
- Adding CTAs that have nothing to do with the topic
- Publishing a comparison when your product is clearly weaker
- Writing a title that promises one thing while the page delivers another

## Key Takeaways

- Aim for conversions, not just clicks. A smaller audience of likely buyers beats a large audience of browsers.
- Keywords with modifiers like "best," "top," "review," "vs," "cheap," and "comparison" are commercial keywords. They signal that the searcher is comparing options before buying.
- Use a tool such as Ahrefs or Semrush to find long-tail keywords with low difficulty and high buying intent, including comparisons, alternatives, and "best of" lists.
- Do not write a comparison article if your product is clearly weaker than the competitor's. It will not convert and it can hurt trust.
- Publish on more than one platform (your website, LinkedIn, YouTube) to widen your ranking surface, especially if your domain is new or your authority is low. Keep each version unique and use canonical URLs where possible.
- Build pages with key takeaways, comparison tables, author profiles, FAQs, and a clear methodology section.
- Information gain wins. Original data and first-hand experience give both Google and AI search engines a reason to cite you.
- Place calls to action that match the reader's intent. Relevant CTAs convert. Spammy CTAs do not.
- Track progress in Google Search Console and treat SEO as a repeated game of getting more shots on net.
