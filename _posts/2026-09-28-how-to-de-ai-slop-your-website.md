Open your homepage in one tab and your closest competitor's in another. Read the first three paragraphs of each, then imagine swapping the logos. If nobody would notice, you have a problem, and it may be one you never meant to create.

That flat, polished, oddly interchangeable voice now has a name: AI slop. On a website it shows up as service pages that promise tailored solutions without naming a single one, blog posts that circle a topic for 1,500 words and land nowhere, and location pages where only the city name changes.

Sometimes an AI tool wrote it. Sometimes it was a freelancer on a deadline, an agency template, or the owner at 11 pm. The source matters less than the result: pages that say nothing specific, prove nothing, and give neither a customer nor a search engine a reason to pick you.

This is a practical cleanup plan for business owners, startups, and local businesses. You will learn how to recognize slop, why it costs more in 2026 than it did a year ago, and how to fix a site page by page without deleting everything and starting over.

## First, a Clarification: Using AI Is Not the Problem

Google's [guidance on generative AI content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"} says it cares whether content is helpful and original, not which tool produced it. What its spam policies target is using automation to generate many pages without adding value for users, a practice it calls scaled content abuse. Careful AI-assisted drafting that a knowledgeable person checks and improves is a different thing entirely.

So treat slop as a quality and honesty problem. A tired human copying a template produces it just as easily as a chatbot does, and the fix is the same either way.

## What AI Slop Looks Like on a Website

Once you know the patterns, you start seeing them everywhere. Here are the most common ones.

| Symptom | Where it shows up | Why it hurts |
|---|---|---|
| Generic claims like best-in-class solutions | Homepage, service pages | Nothing to verify, nothing to remember |
| Filler openers like in today's fast-paced digital world | Blog posts, landing pages | Readers leave before the point arrives |
| Invented specifics: fake statistics, awards, testimonials | Testimonials, case studies | Damages trust and can create legal risk |
| Template location pages where only the city changes | Service area pages | Looks like scaled content or doorway pages |
| Over-hedged answers like it depends on many factors | FAQs, pricing pages | Gives AI answer engines nothing to quote |
| Identical structure on every post | Whole blog | Signals mass production to readers and systems |
| Stock photos of smiling strangers | About and team pages | No real experience on display |

If three or more of these describe your site, keep reading.

## Why This Matters More in 2026

Three things have changed, and they all point the same direction.

**Google keeps tightening spam enforcement.** By our count, the August 2026 spam update, released on August 18, was the third of the year after March and June. Google also updated its [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"} in May 2026 to cover attempts to manipulate generative AI responses. In community reports covered in our [breakdown of the August 2026 spam update](https://f9xr.org/articles/2026/08/19/google-august-2026-spam-update.html), one site with roughly 130 AI-written articles saw daily traffic fall from about 278 visitors to about 20 after the June update. It is one anecdote, not a rule, but it shows how sharply scaled content can be demoted.

**Detection is getting cheaper.** Google Research published a system called SAFE that, according to its authors, helped terminate about 50,000 channel clusters covering 130,000 channels of AI slop over six months. That work targets video platforms, not business websites, but it shows where the industry is heading. We unpack it in our piece on [Google's SAFE system and AI slop](https://f9xr.org/articles/2026/09/26/google-safe-ai-slop-detection-system.html).

**AI search engines need specifics.** ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, and Perplexity pull answers from pages that state concrete facts: names, prices, hours, timelines, processes. A page full of we deliver excellence gives them nothing to cite, so they cite someone else.

And underneath all of that sits the simplest reason. A visitor who senses a page was written to fill space does not call you.

## The 7-Step De-Slop Process

### Step 1: Inventory Every Page and Sort It

Export your URLs from your sitemap or a crawler into a spreadsheet. Add columns for clicks over the last six months from Search Console, enquiries or conversions, last updated date, and who wrote it. Then label each page one of four ways:

- **Keep:** useful, accurate, and specific.
- **Rewrite:** right topic, weak execution.
- **Merge:** overlaps with another page.
- **Delete:** no traffic, no links, no conversions, and nothing worth saving.

Do not delete in bulk on day one. Work in batches so you can see what each change does. Our guide to [reviving dead content with systematic audits](https://f9xr.org/articles/2026/08/20/reviving-dead-content-systematic-audits.html) walks through the audit side in more detail.

### Step 2: Run the Swap Test and the So-What Test

The **swap test**: replace your business name with a competitor's. If the paragraph still reads fine, it is too generic to keep.

The **so what test**: read each sentence and ask so what? If the honest answer is nothing, either cut it or replace it with a fact. Highlight every vague adjective (comprehensive, tailored, seamless, cutting-edge, innovative) and ask what specific thing that word is standing in for.

### Step 3: Add First-Hand Proof

This is the step that turns a page from replaceable into yours. Where your business can offer them, add:

- Real photos of your work, team, and premises, ideally with dates.
- Named people with real credentials and a short bio.
- Prices or price ranges, and realistic timelines such as usually 3 to 5 working days.
- Licenses, registrations, and memberships someone can verify.
- Short case examples, with the client's permission.
- Local detail only a real local business would know.
- Mistakes you regularly see customers make, and how to avoid them.

Google's E-E-A-T framework starts with the first E, experience, for exactly this reason. Our [E-E-A-T checklist](https://f9xr.org/articles/2026/08/04/google-e-e-a-t-checklist-every-seo-should-bookmark.html) shows how to layer these signals across your site.

### Step 4: Fact-Check Every Claim

AI tools can produce confident nonsense: invented statistics, outdated rules, features a product does not have. Check every number, date, regulation, and comparison. Add a source where one exists and delete the claim where you cannot support it.

Be strictest on topics where a mistake hurts people, such as money, health, and legal matters. A tax or compliance page quoting a deadline that changed last year is worse than having no page at all. Also confirm that your hours, address, and phone number match your Google Business Profile exactly, and remove any testimonial you cannot trace to a real customer.

### Step 5: Rewrite for Voice, Not Just Words

Swapping synonyms does not fix slop. The rewrite has to change what the sentence does. A few habits that help:

- Put the answer in the first two sentences.
- Use we and you, and name real things.
- Vary sentence length. Slop tends to march at one steady rhythm.
- Commit to an answer instead of hedging.
- Cut any opener that could start a thousand other articles.

Some phrases are almost always a sign to rewrite: in today's fast-paced world, unlock the power of, game-changer, seamless experience, tailored solutions, and elevate your brand.

Here is what the difference looks like in practice. The bracketed parts are placeholders for your real details.

| Slop | Rewrite |
|---|---|
| "We provide comprehensive, tailored plumbing solutions for all your needs." | "We fix leaking taps, replace geysers, and clear blocked drains. Most calls in [your area] get a visit within [X] hours." |
| "Our expert team is dedicated to excellence in taxation." | "Our two CAs handle GST filing for [number] small businesses. Send us your sales and purchase data by the [date] and we file by the [date]." |
| "In today's fast-paced digital world, having a website is more important than ever." | Delete it. Start with the first useful sentence. |
| "The cost depends on various factors." | "Most jobs cost between [amount] and [amount]. The biggest factors are [factor one] and [factor two]." |

### Step 6: Replace Generic Visuals

Swap stock photos of strangers for real images: your shop, your team, your finished work, screenshots of your process. If you do use stock, choose unbranded, believable images and write descriptive alt text that says what is actually in the picture.

One rule worth holding firmly: never present an AI-generated image as a real employee or a real project. It costs you the trust you are trying to rebuild.

### Step 7: Consolidate Thin and Duplicate Pages

Forty near-identical location pages are one of the fastest ways to look like scaled content. Unless each page carries unique local substance, such as real projects, local pricing, or a named local team, merge them into one strong service area page. Then:

1. Redirect the merged URLs to the best remaining page with a 301 redirect.
2. Update internal links so they point to the new page.
3. Refresh your sitemap and resubmit it in Search Console.

## Keep Slop From Coming Back: A Pre-Publish Checklist

Cleanup fails when the habits that created the problem stay in place. Before anything goes live, ask:

- Does it have a named author with a real bio?
- Does it contain at least one fact only your business could provide?
- Has every number been checked against a source?
- Does the answer appear in the first two sentences?
- Have you read it out loud without cringing?
- Would you be comfortable sending this page to a customer as-is?
- Does it show a genuine last updated date?

### Can You Still Use AI for Drafting?

Yes, with guardrails. Give the tool your real notes, prices, and customer questions instead of a one-line prompt. Ask it for outlines, question lists, or a rough first pass, then rewrite in your own voice and add the proof only you have. The less of your real knowledge goes in, the more slop comes out.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Running text through a humanizer or paraphrasing tool and calling it done.** The emptiness stays, and the wording just gets stranger.
- **Treating AI detectors as a pass or fail test.** They are unreliable and can flag human writing. Judge pages by usefulness and specificity instead.
- **Deleting everything at once.** You lose the pages that were quietly working, and you cannot tell which change caused what.
- **Fixing the words but keeping fake proof.** Invented testimonials and staged team photos undo the whole effort.
- **Letting business details drift.** Inconsistent name, address, and phone details across your site and profiles weaken trust signals. Our [guide to website trust score](https://f9xr.org/articles/2026/08/15/how-to-increase-website-trust-score.html) covers the fundamentals.
- **Expecting overnight recovery.** Google's documentation says recovery from an algorithmic demotion can take months, because its systems need to see sustained change.

## How to Tell If the Cleanup Is Working

| Signal | Where to check | What good looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Clicks and impressions on rewritten pages | Search Console, compare equal date ranges | Gradual upward trend after recrawling |
| Enquiries, calls, form submissions | Your CRM or analytics | More contact from the same traffic |
| Engagement on key pages | Analytics | Longer visits, fewer instant exits |
| Branded searches | Search Console | Steady growth in people searching your name |
| AI answer accuracy | Ask ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity who offers your service in your city | Your business is named and described correctly |

Give changes several weeks before judging them, and change things in batches so the data stays readable.

Google's rater guidelines are the reference point here, not the ranking factor. Sections 4.6.5 and 4.6.6 describe scaled content abuse and main content created with little to no effort, little to no originality, and little to no added value. That language is the closest thing to a written definition of slop you will find, and the [helpful content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"} is the checklist your rewrite is really being measured against.

## Key Takeaways

- AI slop is generic, unverified, mass-produced content, and it can come from a chatbot, a template, or a rushed human.
- Google does not penalize content just because AI helped write it. Its spam policies target scaled, low-value content made to manipulate rankings.
- Use the swap test and the so what test to find pages that say nothing specific.
- First-hand proof such as real photos, named people, prices, and timelines is what makes a page yours.
- Fact-check every claim, especially on money, health, and legal topics, and remove anything you cannot support.
- Merge thin location pages, fix visuals, and work in batches so you can measure what helps.
- Recovery can take months, so build a pre-publish checklist to keep slop from returning.

## Conclusion

Cleaning up AI slop is less about hiding the use of a tool and more about putting your real knowledge back on the page. The businesses that come out ahead are the ones whose pages could only have been written by them: specific, checked, and useful to the person reading.
