If you have ever generated a product image, a social media graphic, or a quick promotional video with Google's Gemini and gotten annoyed by that little sparkle logo stamped in the corner, you are not alone. Enough people complained about it that Google actually did something about it.

![Josh Woodward announcing the Gemini watermark toggle](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HPr8e2CbkAAg0PQ?format=png&name=medium){: width="1104" height="204" loading="lazy"}


On August 14, 2026, Google confirmed a new setting that lets you switch off the visible AI watermark on content created with Gemini. This covers images made with Nano Banana, videos made with Omni, and music made with Lyria, with Search support said to be coming soon. It is a small toggle, but it solves an annoying problem for anyone using Gemini to create client work, marketing assets, or polished business content.

But before you go flipping that switch and assuming your AI-generated content is now indistinguishable from anything else, there is an important catch. The visible watermark disappearing does not mean the content is untraceable. Google is keeping an invisible layer, called SynthID, along with C2PA provenance metadata, embedded in every file regardless of what you choose in settings.

This update matters more than it looks at first glance. Here is what changed, what stays hidden behind the scenes, and how to use it without running into trust or compliance issues.

## What Exactly Changed With the Visible Watermark in Gemini

Here is the update itself, based on Google's own announcement and the reporting that followed.

Google's VP for Gemini, Josh Woodward, [confirmed the change directly on X](https://x.com/joshwoodward/status/2088259242423968162), describing a new Media Watermark setting rolling out across the Gemini app and Google's Flow video editor.

The rollout began August 14, 2026, and Google said it would reach accounts gradually over the following days.

### Where to Find the Toggle

The setting lives under Settings, in a section labeled "Media Watermark." From there, you can switch the visible mark on or off, and your preference applies to all future images, videos, and music tracks you generate.

### What It Covers

* **Nano Banana** (Gemini's image generation model)
* **Omni** (Gemini's video generation model)
* **Lyria** (Gemini's music generation model)

Google has also said support for this toggle is coming to Google Search's AI creation features next, though that had not rolled out at the time of writing.

### Where It Does Not Apply

A few important exceptions to know about.

* **India, South Korea, and Vietnam:** In these markets, the option is currently limited to Google AI Ultra subscribers. Free and standard plan users in these countries keep the visible watermark by default.
* **Work and school accounts:** Google Workspace accounts do not get this setting at all. Visible watermarks stay mandatory regardless of preference.
* **Regions with legal labeling requirements:** In countries where local law requires visible AI labeling on generated content, Google may restrict or override the toggle entirely.

## What Does Not Change: SynthID and C2PA Metadata

This is the part that matters most, and the detail a lot of quick news coverage glosses over. Turning off the visible watermark only removes what you can see. It does not remove the invisible layer of tracking Google has built into every single generation.

### What Is SynthID?

SynthID is [Google DeepMind's imperceptible watermarking technology](https://deepmind.google/technologies/synthid/). It is built into the content itself at the pixel, frame, or audio level, depending on the media type, and it is designed to survive things like screenshots, cropping, resizing, and format conversion. Google has stated that SynthID has now been applied to more than 10 billion pieces of content across its products.

There is no user-facing way to disable SynthID. It is embedded automatically, every time, regardless of your visible watermark setting.

### What Is C2PA Metadata?

C2PA stands for the [Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity](https://contentauthenticity.org/), an industry standard used across parts of the tech and media world to record information about how a piece of digital content was created. Every file Gemini generates carries this metadata, which records its AI origin.

### Why This Matters

Together, these two systems mean that even with the visible watermark switched off, Gemini and Google Search can still confirm whether a piece of content was AI-generated. The transparency has not gone away. It has just moved from something visible on the surface to something detectable underneath.

This is an important distinction for business owners to understand before using this feature for anything client-facing or brand-sensitive. A cleaner-looking image does not mean an untraceable one.

## Why Google Made This Change

According to Woodward, Google gathered direct feedback from users about their biggest frustrations with Gemini, and removing Nano Banana's visible watermark landed in the top 10 requests out of thousands of responses.

The reasoning makes sense from a practical standpoint. A visible watermark is fine on a quick social media post, even useful there. But it becomes a real problem on a client presentation deck, a printed poster, a product mockup, or polished marketing material, where a stray logo in the corner reads as sloppy rather than transparent.

Google's own framing, in Woodward's words, was about striking "a balance between creative control and safety." Businesses get cleaner, more professional-looking output for legitimate creative use, while Google keeps its underlying detection and provenance systems fully intact for accountability purposes.

There is also a competitive angle. Nano Banana competes directly with a growing field of free AI image generators, many of which do not apply visible watermarks at all. A cleaner default output makes Gemini's content easier to use across marketing and creative workflows, closing a real usability gap against competitors.

## Is the Visible Watermark Toggle Good or Bad for Business?

Like most platform changes, the honest answer depends on how you plan to use it.

### The Upside

* **Cleaner, more professional output.** For business owners using Gemini to create marketing graphics, blog images, product mockups, or presentation visuals, removing the watermark means content that looks finished and polished without extra editing steps. For anyone optimizing AI-generated visuals for search, a complete [image SEO audit](https://f9xr.github.io/articles/2026/08/14/image-seo-audit-complete-guide.html) ensures those visuals also perform well in search results.
* **Faster workflows.** No more cropping out corner logos or running images through separate watermark removal tools before publishing.
* **Better client-facing deliverables.** Agencies and freelancers using Gemini for client work can now deliver assets that do not visibly announce their AI origin, which matters for professional presentation.

### The Catch

* **Traceability has not disappeared.** Because SynthID and C2PA metadata remain embedded, content can still be identified as AI-generated by detection tools, platforms, and increasingly by Google Search itself. If your brand values full transparency, or operates in a regulated industry, this is worth factoring into your content policy.
* **Trust and disclosure considerations.** Just because you can hide the visible mark does not mean you should skip disclosing AI use where it matters, particularly for testimonials, before-and-after imagery, or anything that could mislead a customer if presented as authentic, non-AI content.
* **Platform and regional inconsistency.** Since the rollout varies by account type and region, check settings across your team's accounts to make sure the watermark preference matches what each piece of content needs.

## Practical Tips for Using This Responsibly

Here is how business owners can make the most of this update without running into trust or compliance issues.

### 1. Use It for Legitimate Creative Polish, Not Deception

Turning off the watermark is great for internal decks, blog graphics, and general marketing visuals. It is not a tool for making AI content pass as something it is not in contexts where authenticity matters, like customer testimonials or real product photography.

### 2. Keep a Disclosure Practice for Sensitive Content

Even with the visible mark gone, consider adding your own disclosure language where appropriate, especially for content touching on health, finance, legal topics, or anything where AI generation should be clear to your audience.

### 3. Check Settings Across Your Team

If multiple people on your team generate content through Gemini, do not assume everyone has the same watermark preference set. Standardize your team's approach based on what each piece of content is actually used for.

### 4. Understand That Detection Tools Still Work

Do not rely on the absence of a visible watermark as proof your content cannot be identified as AI-generated. SynthID and C2PA metadata mean detection remains possible, and increasingly, Google Search itself may start surfacing this information.

### 5. Watch for the Search Rollout

Since Google has said this feature is coming to Search's AI creation tools next, keep an eye on how that expands, especially if you use Google's AI features directly within Search for content creation.

### 6. Combine AI-Generated Visuals With Original Brand Assets

For the strongest, most trustworthy brand presence, mix AI-generated visuals with real photography, original graphics, and authentic behind-the-scenes content. This builds customer trust that a clean AI image alone cannot fully replicate.
