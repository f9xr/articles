Microsoft Advertising automated bidding changed on 18 November 2026. The company rebuilt the prediction model that decides what each click is worth, and it says the new version meets CPA and ROAS targets more consistently, reacts to target changes almost twice as fast and spreads budget more evenly through the day.

The numbers in the announcement are Microsoft's own averages: about 10% more conversion value per click, 8% more conversion value for advertisers moving from Target CPA to Target ROAS, and 18% more conversions for those moving from Enhanced CPC to Maximize Conversions with a CPA target. Your results can land above or below any of them.

For a local business spending $1,500 a month, the operational details matter more than the headline. A smarter bidder still needs usable conversion data, a strategy that matches how you sell and a page worth landing on. Here is what changed, how to read the numbers and what to do this week.

---

## What Microsoft Advertising Automated Bidding Changed

The Microsoft Advertising automated bidding update comes from Carlo Buchmann, a principal product manager at the company. In the [company's announcement](https://about.ads.microsoft.com/en/blog/post/november-2026/building-the-next-generation-of-automated-bidding-at-microsoft-advertising), he calls it the biggest upgrade to bidding models in a decade. Every automated bid rests on a prediction: how likely is this click to become a sale, and how much will that sale bring in. Microsoft says it rebuilt that prediction with new user signals and a better way of valuing each conversion.

Three changes matter in practice:

1. **More consistent performance.** Bids should hit your CPA and ROAS targets more reliably without giving up conversion volume or value.
2. **Faster optimization.** Change a target, and bids adjust nearly twice as quickly as before.
3. **More predictable delivery.** Budgets pace more evenly across the day, and campaigns reach full budget sooner.

This Microsoft Ads bidding update is already live in its first stage. Microsoft says it will keep pushing improvements to more accounts automatically, with more planned for 2027.

There is no switch to flip. The question is whether your account can benefit from the change. If you missed the earlier [AI Max global rollout](https://f9xr.org/articles/2026/08/20/microsoft-advertising-ai-max-global-rollout.html), that is a separate update, though both can affect the same campaigns from different directions.

## Why a Small Budget Feels This More Than a Large One

Large advertisers can absorb a bad week. A clinic spending $1,500 a month cannot. One wasteful stretch takes a visible bite out of the marketing budget, and the owner notices before the month ends.

Automated bidding reads signals such as device, location and the time of day, then sets a bid for every auction in real time. Query context and audience data join in as well. No person can do that at scale.

When the prediction improves, a small account notices in a few places. Less money goes to clicks that were never going to convert. Results become steadier, which makes planning possible. And the account needs less babysitting, which matters when you also run the business.

Budget pacing is the other half of the change. If the old model spent most of a daily budget by lunchtime, the afternoon auctions were lost. Even pacing does not make a $1,500 budget larger, but it keeps the campaign present when customers search after work.

The same logic cuts the other way. Automation also repeats your mistakes faster. If your conversion goal counts a contact page view as a lead, a better bidder gets better at finding people who view the contact page and leave. Fix the signal before you celebrate the model.

## The Three Numbers, With the Fine Print

| Change you make | Reported average result | Data window |
|---|---|---|
| Use Maximize Conversion Value or Target ROAS with the new models | About 10% more conversion value per click at a similar ROAS | August 2026, global, across Search and the Audience Network |
| Move Search campaigns from Target CPA to Target ROAS | About 8% more conversion value at a similar ROAS | August to September 2026, global |
| Move from Enhanced CPC to Maximize Conversions with a CPA target | About 18% more conversions at a similar cost per action | June to July 2026, global |

Read the phrase "at a similar ROAS" first. It means efficiency stayed roughly flat while value or volume rose, which is the outcome you want.

Averages hide the spread. Some advertisers will see larger lifts, and some will see none. Microsoft pulled these figures from its own platform across windows in June to September 2026, and it published no account counts or confidence intervals. Treat each one as a reason to test, not a forecast.

The strongest gains come from value-based bidding, where you send actual transaction values instead of counting every lead as one unit. If you sell services at different prices, that extra data is worth the setup. ## What to Do With Your Account This Week

The rollout is automatic, so your job is preparation rather than installation. Six steps cover most of it.

Write down a baseline first. Note the last 30 days of spend, conversions, CPA and ROAS for the campaign you plan to change. Without that snapshot, you cannot tell whether the new model helped or the season did.

### Start with the conversion signal

Automated bidding optimizes toward whatever you tell it a conversion is. Microsoft names Universal Event Tracking (UET), Conversions API and offline import as the three tracking routes. It recommends [UET](https://help.ads.microsoft.com/#apex/ads/en/56913/2) because it sends richer, more granular signals. [Conversions API](https://help.ads.microsoft.com/#apex/ads/en/60239/2) adds coverage from apps and CRM systems, plus store data, in real time.

For a local business, count the actions that produce revenue: phone calls and form submissions, plus paid orders. Keep page views and button clicks out of the count. Test every goal yourself before you trust it, and upload closed offline sales so the bidder learns which clicks turned into money.

### Choose a strategy your data can support

The rule is simpler than most accounts make it. If the account already has at least five conversions, start new campaigns on a conversion-based strategy. Conversion history sits at the account level, so every campaign can draw on it. If the account is new and below five, start on Enhanced CPC, let it collect history, then move to Maximize Conversions with a CPA target or Maximize Conversion Value with a ROAS target.

When the choice is not obvious:

- Every lead is worth roughly the same, as with a service call: use Maximize Conversions, with or without a CPA target.
- Sales vary in price, as with an online store or tiered services: use Maximize Conversion Value or Target ROAS, and pass real revenue values.

| Topic | Common old habit | What Microsoft now recommends |
|---|---|---|
| Starting a new campaign | Begin with Enhanced CPC to be safe | Start conversion-based once the account has five or more conversions |
| Brand-new account | Jump straight to Target CPA | Use Enhanced CPC to collect history, then graduate |
| Changing targets | Slash the CPA or double the ROAS overnight | Move in steps of about 15% and review after one conversion cycle |
| Bid limits | Keep a Maximum CPC cap as a safety net | Remove it, since it can conflict with your target |
| Judging results | Check keyword CPCs after a few days | Judge at campaign or portfolio level by conversions and CPA or ROAS |
| Sample size | Decide after five or six conversions | Aim for roughly 30 conversions in 30 days |
| Tracking | Rely on one source | Use UET, and consider Conversions API and offline import alongside it |

### Move targets in 15% steps

Targets can change whenever you like, and the system keeps learning. Big moves cause volatility. Microsoft lists new campaigns and strategy switches, along with changes to conversion goals, as the changes most likely to shake performance. A large CPA increase or ROAS decrease opens new inventory, so expect a settling period.

If you want a calm account, move in 15% steps and wait one full conversion cycle before you judge the result. A conversion cycle is the time your business usually takes to turn a click into a sale or a lead.

A $40 target CPA, raised by 15%, becomes $46. Wait a cycle, review, then decide the next step.

That approach assumes a target behaves like a hard wall. Microsoft describes targets as directional levers instead.

According to the company, campaigns can beat a CPA or ROAS target even when the budget limits delivery, so the target steers volume and efficiency rather than capping them. Test the claim on your own numbers. The [bid strategy help page](https://help.ads.microsoft.com/#apex/ads/en/56786/1) explains how each strategy treats a target.

### Delete the Maximum CPC cap

A Maximum CPC cap overrides your CPA or ROAS target. Microsoft says the two settings send conflicting instructions, even when the cap sits above your average CPC. Remove it and control spend with the levers tied to outcomes: budget and bid targets, plus conversion value rules.

### Judge at campaign level, after 30 conversions

The guideline is about 30 conversions in 30 days, plus one or two conversion cycles after a major change. Smaller samples produce noise that hides real performance. Read results at campaign or portfolio level, not at ad group or keyword level, and judge by conversions and CPA or ROAS rather than keyword CPC.

If 30 conversions a month feels far away, consolidate small campaigns so the data is not spread thin. Check that your goals track meaningful outcomes. Use portfolio-level evaluation for related campaigns, as Microsoft suggests, and avoid reading a decision into a six-click keyword.

### Fix the page the click lands on

Better bidding brings more of the right people to your site. It cannot fix a page that loads slowly, hides the phone number or buries the offer. Speed, a clear next step, visible contact details and proof such as reviews all matter more once traffic quality improves. If the site is the weak link, start with [why a small business needs a website](https://f9xr.org/articles/2026/10/22/why-small-business-needs-website-2026.html) and treat paid search as the second step.

Teams like F9XR spend most of a paid search project on this half of the work: conversion tracking, landing page structure and the local details that make a business easy to choose. Bidding buys the visit. The site earns the inquiry. That is also why Microsoft's [rules for AI-generated ads](https://f9xr.org/articles/2026/09/14/microsoft-advertising-ai-generated-ads-rules.html) matter here, since ad quality and landing page quality get judged together.

## Mistakes That Quietly Cost You Money

- Changing a campaign and its strategy, plus its targets, in the same week. You lose the ability to tell which change did what.
- Counting weak actions as conversions. If a contact page view counts the same as a booked appointment, the bidder chases browsers.
- Judging after three days. Learning takes time, and small samples mislead.
- Mixing a CPC cap with a CPA target. The instructions can fight each other.
- Ignoring seasonality. Planned promotions and realistic peak budgets give the system useful context.
- Sending paid traffic to a page nobody has tested on a phone.

## Key Takeaways

- The rollout is automatic. Microsoft says the first stage is live and more improvements arrive in 2027, so there is nothing to switch on.
- The gains are internal averages: 10% more value per click, 8% more value from Target CPA to Target ROAS, and 18% more conversions from Enhanced CPC to Maximize Conversions.
- Tracking decides what the bidder learns from. Set up UET first, then extend coverage with Conversions API or offline imports if sales close away from the site.
- Start conversion-based bidding once the account has five conversions. Below that, run Enhanced CPC until the history exists.
- Move targets in 15% steps, drop the Maximum CPC cap and wait for 30 conversions in 30 days before you judge a change.

## Conclusion

Before you touch a bid, export your current settings and list every conversion action in the account. Mark the ones that represent revenue, then pause or fix the rest. Pick one campaign, move its target by 15% or switch it to a value-based strategy, and leave it alone for six weeks.

Microsoft's figures come from its own data, and the rollout will keep changing through 2027. Your account history is the only test that matters.
