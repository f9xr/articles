If you run Search campaigns through Microsoft Advertising, or you've been thinking about diversifying beyond Google Ads, there's a change worth paying attention to. On August 19, 2026, Microsoft made AI Max, its AI-driven suite of Search campaign tools, generally available across every advertiser account worldwide.

This isn't a small settings tweak. AI Max changes how your ads get matched to search queries, how your ad copy gets written, and where your traffic can land, all powered by AI working across Bing, Copilot, and Microsoft's broader search ecosystem. For a lot of business owners, this could mean more visibility for less manual keyword work. For others, it raises real questions about control, budget predictability, and brand safety.

## What Is AI Max and What Changed on August 19

AI Max isn't brand new. Microsoft first announced it back on April 21, 2026, and opened it up for pilot testing in May. What happened on August 19 is that Microsoft moved AI Max from a limited, opt-in pilot into general availability, meaning it's now accessible across every Microsoft Advertising account globally, with a set of advertiser controls Microsoft had promised but hadn't fully delivered until now.

### The Three Core Features

AI Max adds three main automation capabilities on top of existing Search campaigns:

1. **Search term matching** - This lets your ads reach queries beyond your actual keyword list, using signals pulled from your keywords, ad copy, landing pages, and inferred user intent. Microsoft says this is specifically built to help ads appear for longer, more conversational searches, including ones happening directly inside Bing and Copilot.
2. **Text customization** - AI Max can generate additional ad messaging automatically, using your existing ad assets and your website content as source material, rather than requiring you to manually write every variation yourself.
3. **URL expansion** - This allows Microsoft's system to send traffic to the most relevant page on your site for a given query, even if that page isn't the exact final URL you originally set in your ad.

### What's New With This Rollout Specifically

The general availability rollout brought a handful of advertiser controls that weren't part of the original pilot:

* **Brand inclusions and exclusions** - control over which brand names can or can't appear in the expanded search matching
* **Term exclusions for text asset generation** - the ability to block specific words from showing up in AI-generated ad copy, similar in spirit to a negative keyword list but applied to generated text itself
* **URL rules** - the ability to limit exactly where URL expansion is allowed to send traffic, keeping AI-driven landing page selection within boundaries you set

Microsoft's Ads Liaison, Navah Hopkins, said directly that advertiser feedback shaped the decision to ship these controls at launch rather than after the fact, calling them a critical tool advertisers specifically asked for.

## Will AI Max Turn On Automatically in Your Account

This is a common point of confusion, so it's worth clarifying directly.

If you're already using Predictive Matching or autogenerated text assets in an existing campaign, Microsoft is folding those features under the AI Max umbrella, and the corresponding AI Max setting will be enabled automatically in your account. You may notice this without having made any change yourself.

However, the remaining AI Max features, meaning search term matching's expanded scope and the newer controls, will not activate unless you specifically opt in. Microsoft has also confirmed that AI Max settings can carry over automatically when advertisers import existing campaigns from Google Ads, so if you're running parallel campaigns across both platforms, it's worth checking your settings after any import.

**Practical tip:** Log into your Microsoft Advertising account and check the settings panel on any active Search campaign. If AI Max is available to your account, you'll see it listed as a campaign option directly.

## Why Microsoft Is Pushing AI Max So Hard Right Now

There's a business context behind this rollout that's worth understanding, because it explains both the timing and the intensity of Microsoft's push.

### Search Revenue Growth Is Slowing

Microsoft's fiscal fourth quarter results, reported July 29, 2026, showed search advertising revenue, excluding traffic acquisition costs, up 10% year over year, or 9% in constant currency. That's the second consecutive quarter of deceleration compared to the 21% growth rate Microsoft was posting as recently as fiscal Q3 2025. Total company revenue for the quarter reached 90 billion dollars, but search remains the smallest, and arguably most exposed, line item as query behavior shifts toward conversational AI interfaces.

### Conversational Search Doesn't Automatically Follow Keyword Lists

Traditional keyword-based advertising was built for a search box, not a conversation. As more queries happen through [Copilot and similar AI-assisted interfaces](https://f9xr.github.io/articles/2026/08/22/chatgpt-ads-guide-business-owners.html), a fixed keyword list simply can't capture the full range of ways people are now phrasing what they want. Search term matching is essentially an inventory expansion mechanism, opening up auction opportunities for queries that a traditional keyword list would have missed entirely, without requiring any new users to show up.

### The Agentic Web Framing

At Microsoft Advertising's Activate 2026 event in May, the company laid out a framework describing three concurrent eras of the web running in parallel: a "help me find it" era of traditional search results, a "help me choose" era where AI assistants synthesize options for users, and a "do it for me" agentic era where AI agents complete transactions directly on a person's behalf. Microsoft cited a striking data point at that event, agentic web traffic reportedly grew 7,851% year over year, based on the HUMAN Security 2026 State of AI Traffic & Cyberthreat Benchmark Report. AI Max is Microsoft's tool for staying relevant across all three of these search behaviors at once.

### A Deadline Behind the Scenes

There's also a technical deadline worth knowing about if you or your agency manages campaigns programmatically. Microsoft Advertising is retiring its legacy SOAP API by January 31, 2027, and new features are becoming exclusive to the REST API starting October 1, 2026. Any AI Max controls exposed through the API after that date will only be available through REST, meaning tools or scripts still built on SOAP will lose access to the newest settings.

## Is AI Max Good or Bad for Your Business

### The Case For Turning It On

* **Expanded reach without manual keyword expansion work.** Search term matching can surface your ads for relevant queries you'd never have thought to add as keywords yourself.
* **Cost efficiency for small businesses.** Based on Microsoft's own internal pilot data, early adopters saw roughly a 5 percent improvement in click-through rate and about an 8 percent incremental conversion lift. Microsoft Advertising's cost-per-click has also historically run lower than Google Ads in many verticals, meaning ad dollars can stretch further.
* **Advertiser controls shipped at launch, not after.** Unlike some past automated ad features that launched with limited guardrails, Microsoft included brand exclusions, term exclusions, and URL rules right from general availability, directly in response to advertiser demand.
* **Positioning for where search is heading.** Whether or not you love the idea of conversational, AI-driven search, it's clearly where query behavior is trending, and getting familiar with AI Max now means less catch-up later.

### The Case for Caution

* **Reduced predictability.** Expanded search term matching means your ads can show up for queries you didn't explicitly choose, which requires closer monitoring of search term reports than a traditional, tightly controlled keyword campaign.
* **AI-generated ad copy needs oversight.** Text customization is genuinely useful, but any AI-generated messaging should be reviewed regularly against your actual brand voice and claims, especially in regulated industries.
* **A toggle isn't proof of performance.** As some industry analysts have pointed out, shipping controls at launch doesn't by itself prove the underlying matching and generation systems perform well. Early results look promising, but it's still worth testing carefully with your own account rather than assuming universal success.
* **New audience network changes to watch.** Separately, Microsoft has opened its Audience Network to vitamin and supplement ads as of July 1, 2026, alongside licensed gambling ads, while introducing a global ban on clinical trial ads starting August 1, 2026. If you're in an affected category, review these policy shifts alongside your AI Max settings.

## How to Get Started With AI Max Responsibly

Here's a practical rollout plan for business owners ready to test AI Max without losing control of their campaigns.

### 1. Check Your Account Settings First
Confirm whether AI Max is already visible in your Search campaign settings. If you were previously using Predictive Matching or autogenerated text assets, check whether those settings have already been folded into AI Max automatically.

### 2. Set Up Your Guardrails Before Turning On Expanded Features
Before opting into the broader search term matching or text customization features, configure your brand inclusions and exclusions, your term exclusions for generated text, and your URL rules. Setting these boundaries first prevents surprises later.

### 3. Start With One Campaign, Not Your Whole Account
Test AI Max on a single, well-established Search campaign first. This gives you a clean comparison point against your existing performance data before rolling it out account-wide.

### 4. Review Search Term Reports Weekly
Since expanded matching can bring in queries outside your original keyword list, check your [search term reports](https://f9xr.github.io/articles/2026/08/23/sem-metrics-complete-guide-2026.html) regularly, at least weekly during the first month, to catch anything irrelevant or off-brand early.

### 5. Audit AI-Generated Ad Copy Regularly
Set a recurring reminder to review the ad text AI Max is generating. Confirm it stays consistent with your actual offers, pricing, and brand tone, particularly if you operate in a regulated or sensitive industry.

### 6. Migrate Off the SOAP API If You're Still Using It
If your agency or internal team manages Microsoft Ads campaigns through automation scripts or third-party tools, confirm those tools are moving to the REST API well ahead of the October 1, 2026 cutoff for new feature access.

## What This Means for Local Businesses and Startups

If you're a local business or a startup working with a lean marketing budget, this rollout is genuinely worth your attention, and not just as background industry news.

Microsoft Advertising has historically offered a real cost-per-click advantage over Google Ads in many verticals, simply because there's less competition bidding on the same keywords. AI Max, with its expanded search term matching, could help small businesses capture relevant traffic they'd never have manually built keyword lists for, particularly around longer, more specific, conversational search phrases that increasingly show up on Bing and Copilot.

That said, local businesses in particular should lean into the new advertiser controls right away rather than turning on every AI Max feature blindly. Brand exclusions and URL rules matter a lot for a business with a narrow, well-defined service area and a specific reputation to protect. Getting those guardrails set correctly from day one is worth the extra ten minutes of setup time.

For startups looking to diversify ad spend beyond Google, this rollout is a reasonable moment to test Microsoft Advertising seriously, particularly if your audience skews toward professional, B2B, or Windows-based user bases where Bing and Copilot usage tends to run higher.
