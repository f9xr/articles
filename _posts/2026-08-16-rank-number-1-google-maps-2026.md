## The Three Official Pillars: Relevance, Distance, Prominence

Google has stated directly that local rankings are based on three core factors. [How Google determines local ranking](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"} is spelled out in the official help documentation, and everything else you'll read about Google Maps SEO fits under one of these three umbrellas.

### Relevance

How well your Google Business Profile matches what someone searched for. This is shaped by your business categories, how complete your profile is, and how well your website content actually reflects your services.

### Distance

How far your business is from the person searching, or from the location included in their search. This is the one pillar you genuinely cannot control directly, though choosing accurate service areas and location settings still matters.

### Prominence

How well known and well regarded your business is, both online and offline. This covers reviews, backlinks, citations, and general brand visibility across the web.

## Step by Step: How to Actually Rank Higher

### 1. Nail Your Primary Category First

This is consistently identified as the single most impactful, fastest moving factor available. Choosing the correct, most specific primary category can gain a business 2 to 4 positions within about three weeks. A generic or slightly mismatched category is one of the most common, easily fixed mistakes local businesses make.

**Practical tip:** Search for your ideal customer's exact query and note what categories your top ranking competitors are using. Match your primary category as precisely as possible, and add relevant secondary categories to expand your reach without diluting your core relevance.

### 2. Complete Every Single Field on Your Profile

Businesses with complete profiles are consistently preferred by consumers, and Google treats completeness as a direct relevance signal. That means filling out your business description, all applicable attributes, service lists, and accurate hours, not just the bare minimum.

**Practical tip:** Treat your services list like a menu, not an afterthought. When Google can read exactly what you offer in structured fields instead of guessing from vague descriptions, it can match you to far more specific, relevant searches.

### 3. Fix Your NAP Consistency Everywhere

Consistent name, address, and phone number data across your Google Business Profile, website, and citations yields a measurable ranking boost, with one 2026 analysis putting the improvement at 31% for multi location businesses specifically. Inconsistent NAP data actively confuses Google's verification systems and can suppress your visibility. For the full audit method and the numbers behind it, see our [information consistency audit guide for SEO, AEO, and GEO](https://f9xr.github.io/articles/2026/08/13/consistency-audits-seo-aeo-geo.html){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"}.

### 4. Build a Genuine Review Strategy, Not Just a Review Count

This is where a lot of businesses get the strategy wrong. Total review count matters less than most owners assume, review velocity, a steady, consistent flow of new reviews, consistently outperforms a single burst of reviews followed by silence. Listings with 50 or more reviews and a 4.5 star or higher rating see a 61% higher chance of ranking in the top spots, and one 2026 analysis found businesses with 100 or more reviews ranking 3.2 times higher on average.

**Practical tip:** Aim for a steady pace of roughly 5 to 10 new reviews per month rather than trying to gather 50 reviews in a single week. Respond to every review, positive and negative, professionally and promptly; response rate itself is a signal Google tracks.

### 5. Post Regularly on Your Profile

Google Business Profile posts aren't just a marketing nicety, profiles that post regularly appear in the top three map results 3.1 times more often than profiles that don't. This is one of the lowest effort, most underused levers available.

**Practical tip:** Even a simple weekly post, an update, an offer, a recent project photo, meaningfully outperforms an abandoned profile that hasn't been touched since setup.

### 6. Add Real, Recent Photos

Photos directly affect engagement and, by extension, the behavioral signals Google factors into rankings. Businesses that upload photos regularly, actual location photos, team photos, and completed work, rather than stock imagery, tend to see stronger engagement and dwell time.

### 7. Strengthen Your Website's Local Relevance

Your Google Business Profile doesn't operate in isolation, Google evaluates your business as a full entity, profile and website together. Dedicated service pages, location specific content, and clear geographic keyword relevance on your actual site all reinforce what your profile claims.

**Practical tip:** If you serve multiple areas or neighborhoods, build a genuinely useful, unique page for each rather than duplicating the same generic content with the city name swapped out. Thin, duplicated location pages tend to underperform or get filtered out entirely.

### 8. Earn Genuine Local Citations and Backlinks

Accurate listings on relevant local directories, industry associations, and chamber of commerce sites reinforce prominence. Quality backlinks from real, relevant local or industry sources carry meaningfully more weight than a large volume of low quality directory submissions. ### 9. Encourage the Behavioral Signals That Prove You're Worth Ranking

Click through rate, click to call actions, and direction requests all feed into Google's understanding of whether your listing is actually satisfying searchers. The average local pack click through rate for the top three positions sits around 43%, which is exactly why the factors above compound on each other, a stronger profile earns more clicks, which reinforces the ranking further.

## Mistakes That Quietly Sabotage Your Ranking

- **Keyword stuffing your business name.** Adding service terms or location names to your business name beyond your actual legal name is against Google's guidelines and risks suspension, erasing all prior progress.
- **Treating Google Business Profile and your website as separate projects.** Google evaluates your full entity together; neglecting either one weakens the other.
- **Using a virtual office or mailbox address for a service area business.** This violates Google's guidelines and can lead to suspension once flagged.
- **Creating duplicate listings for a single location.** This confuses ranking systems rather than boosting visibility, and typically backfires.
- **Ignoring negative reviews or responding defensively.** A professional, calm response to a negative review is itself a [trust signal](https://f9xr.github.io/articles/2026/08/15/how-to-increase-website-trust-score.html){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"}; silence or defensiveness is not.
- **Abandoning the profile after initial setup.** Profiles that go untouched for months lose the behavioral and freshness signals that active, engaged profiles benefit from.
