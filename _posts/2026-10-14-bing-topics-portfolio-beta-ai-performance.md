The idea is plain: it takes the individual searches where your site already earns AI citations and groups them into broad topics, so you can see what your site is known for, how crowded each subject is, and where competitors get cited while you do not.

**Quick answer:** Topics Portfolio is a beta tab in Bing Webmaster Tools' AI Performance report that groups the grounding queries where your site appears into broad topics. For each topic it shows how much AI search demand exists, how many sites are cited, and how many queries sit inside it. It also lists opportunity queries, which are searches where other sites get cited and yours does not.

## What Is Topics Portfolio in Bing Webmaster Tools?

Topics Portfolio is a new view inside the [AI Performance report](https://blogs.bing.com/webmaster/2026/2/Introducing-AI-Performance-in-Bing-Webmaster-Tools-Public-Preview/){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"}, the dashboard that shows how your site contributes to AI generated answers. It takes the grounding queries where your site already shows up and sorts them into thematic clusters. Microsoft's own examples of clusters are broad subjects like football, taxes, or AI chatbots.

So instead of scanning hundreds of separate queries, you get a short list of topics that describes what your site is known for in AI search.

### Wait, what are grounding queries?

When an AI system answers a question, it often runs its own searches behind the scenes to find sources it can trust. Bing calls those behind the scenes searches grounding queries. When your page is used as a source for one of them, your site earns a citation. More citations across more relevant queries usually means more visibility inside AI answers.

Microsoft is clearly investing in giving site owners real data about AI search, something Google Search Console does not yet offer in the same depth. That gap makes Topics Portfolio the most useful free AI visibility data most small sites can get right now.

## How Topics Portfolio Works

If your site has enough data, you will see a new **Topics Portfolio** tab next to Citations and Pages inside the AI Performance report. Smaller sites may not see it yet, and the beta section below explains why.

### The main portfolio table

The landing page lists topics where your site has a meaningful presence. Each topic comes with aggregate measures that describe what is happening beyond your own site.

| Column | What it tells you | Why you should care |
|---|---|---|
| **Topic** | A broad cluster of related grounding queries where your site appears | Shows what AI systems associate with your brand |
| **Topic Size** | The approximate total citations across all sites for that topic in the chosen period | A rough signal of how much AI search demand the topic has |
| **Sites Cited** | The approximate number of sites appearing for queries in that topic | Tells you how crowded the topic is |
| **Queries** | The approximate number of distinct grounding queries in the topic | Shows how wide or narrow the topic is |

### Top opportunity queries

Below the main table, Bing lists the strongest queries across your whole portfolio where other sites are cited and yours is not. Citation volumes here appear as ranges, not exact numbers. Treat the list as a quick, cross topic view of places where you could grow.

### Inside a single topic

Click any topic and you get a detail page with three summary cards: the topic's total size, your own citations within it, and the number of queries it contains. Under those cards sit two tables.

- **Your grounding queries:** queries in this topic where your site already gets cited. These line up with what you see in Citations and Pages.
- **Opportunity queries:** queries in the same topic where other sites appear and yours does not.

That split is the useful part. One side shows what you already own. The other shows what is still open.

You can view the data across a one, two, or three month window. Every topic row and every opportunity query also has thumbs up and thumbs down buttons, so you can tell Bing when a classification looks right or wrong.

## Why This Matters for Small and Local Businesses

Most small business owners ask the same thing: how do I know if AI tools are even mentioning my business? Until recently the honest answer was to type questions into ChatGPT, Gemini, or Perplexity and hope. Topics Portfolio does not replace that kind of spot checking, but it gives you something structured and repeatable.

Here is what it can do for you in practice:

1. Show your real AI footprint. You may find AI systems link you to a topic you barely target, or ignore one you consider your specialty.
2. Reveal crowded versus open topics. A topic with high demand and few cited sites is a very different opportunity from one with huge demand and hundreds of competing sites.
3. Feed your content calendar. Opportunity queries are real searches AI systems are running, which beats guessing.
4. Support smarter competitive positioning. You can see where others earn citations and decide whether to compete head on or pick a niche angle.

### A quick example (hypothetical)

Say you run a dental clinic. Your portfolio might show a topic like teeth whitening where you have a few citations, and an opportunity query such as "is teeth whitening safe for sensitive teeth" where several competitors are cited and you are not. That tells you what to write: a clear, medically reviewed page that answers the question directly, with a named dentist as the author.

Or take an online shoe store. You might see a topic around running shoes with a lot of demand and many sites cited. Rather than chasing the broad head term, you could target a narrower opportunity query like running shoes for flat feet, where a well structured buying guide can stand out.

These examples show how to read the data. They are not real figures from any Bing account.

## Understanding the Beta: Limits You Should Know

Microsoft was upfront that this is a beta. The data is built on AI generated classification and aggregate measures, so treat it as a directional planning signal, not an exact ledger. Here are the main limits Bing called out, and how we suggest handling each one.

| Limitation | What it means | How to handle it |
|---|---|---|
| **Classification is AI generated** | A query may land in a topic you would have labeled differently | Use thumbs down feedback, and focus on the pattern rather than a single query |
| **Low data topics are hidden** | Topics with too little site presence or activity are not shown, so smaller sites may see a short list or no tab at all | Keep publishing quality content and check back monthly |
| **Branded queries can appear as opportunities** | A competitor's brand term may show up as an opportunity for you | Skip branded terms that you cannot legitimately target |
| **Numbers are approximate** | Topic Size, Sites Cited, and Queries are estimates, and opportunity citations appear as ranges | Compare topics against each other instead of chasing exact values |

**Our take:** Treat Topics Portfolio the way you would treat a weather forecast. It will not tell you exactly what happens, but it is far better than walking outside blind.

## How to Read the Numbers: A Simple Prioritization Framework

Bing does not prescribe a scoring system, so this is our own working framework for turning the three measures into decisions. Use it as a starting point.

| Pattern you see | Likely meaning | Suggested move |
|---|---|---|
| High Topic Size, few Sites Cited | Real demand, less competition | Top priority. Build in depth content here |
| High Topic Size, many Sites Cited | Big demand, crowded field | Go narrow. Target specific opportunity queries and local angles |
| Low Topic Size, few Sites Cited | Small but easy topic | Quick wins. Answer these with short focused pages |
| Low Topic Size, many Sites Cited | Little demand, lots of noise | Low priority unless it is core to your brand |
| Many of your own citations already | A strength | Protect it. Refresh content and add proof of expertise |
| Zero citations on a topic you care about | A gap | Audit the page and rewrite it to answer questions directly |

## Step by Step: How to Use Topics Portfolio

Bing's own guidance is simple: sign in, open AI Performance for your site, pick the topics view, choose a window, start with topics where your citations are already concentrated, then work down the opportunity queries. Here is how we would build on that.

### Step 1: Open the report

Sign in to [Bing Webmaster Tools](https://www.bing.com/webmasters){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"}, choose your verified site, and open **AI Performance**. If you have not verified your site yet, do that first. If you do not see the Topics Portfolio tab, your site may not have enough data yet.

### Step 2: Start with your strongest topics

Look at where your citations are already concentrated. These are the subjects AI systems already trust you for. Make sure the pages behind them are accurate, current, and clearly authored.

### Step 3: Check the aggregate columns

Compare Topic Size, Sites Cited, and Queries for each topic using the framework above. Mark which topics have high demand and which are crowded.

### Step 4: Open the detail page

Click into your top three or four topics. Compare your grounding queries against the opportunity queries.

### Step 5: Turn opportunity queries into a content plan

For every opportunity query that fits your business, decide whether it needs a new page, a new section on an existing page, or a rewrite. Use a table like this one:

| Topic | Opportunity query | Action | Page type | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Example topic | Example question | Create new page | FAQ or guide | High |
| Example topic | Example question | Add section to existing page | Service page | Medium |
| Example topic | Example question | Rewrite intro and add schema | Blog post | Medium |

### Step 6: Write for answers, not just keywords

AI systems pick pages that answer questions clearly. Put a direct, one or two sentence answer near the top of the section, then add detail, examples, and proof. Our [AEO landing page optimization guide](https://f9xr.org/articles/2026/08/23/aeo-landing-page-optimization-guide.html) walks through this approach.

### Step 7: Measure again after four to eight weeks

Come back, compare the same topics, and note whether citations moved. Because the data is approximate, look for trends over several weeks, not day to day swings.

## Bing Is Only One Lens: Keep the Bigger Picture

Here is the honest caveat. Topics Portfolio shows activity from Bing's own AI search grounding. ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Perplexity, and Google's AI features each use their own mix of sources and methods, and those can change without notice. So this data tells you what is happening on Bing, and it is a strong hint about general AI visibility, but it is not a full map of every AI platform.

Use Bing's data alongside your Google Search Console numbers, manual checks in the major AI tools, and your own analytics. Our guide on [monitoring AI search trends to boost visibility and citations](https://f9xr.org/articles/2026/08/26/monitor-ai-search-trends-boost-visibility-citations.html) covers a practical routine for this, and Google's own [Search Central documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"} is the reference to keep open when you combine the two.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. Treating beta numbers as exact. They are approximate by design.
2. Targeting branded opportunity queries. You cannot win on a competitor's brand name and you should not try.
3. Writing thin pages for every opportunity. One strong page beats five weak ones.
4. Ignoring accuracy. AI systems favor reliable sources. A wrong claim can cost you trust.
5. Skipping feedback. The thumbs buttons help Bing improve the classification, and better classification helps you.
6. Only checking once. AI search shifts quickly. Make this a monthly habit.
