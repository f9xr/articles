F9XR Articles accepts guest contributions from people who can show they have done the work, not just read about it. If you have run the migration, held the budget, or shipped the thing, we want to hear your version of it.

We do not pay for contributions. What you get is a byline, distribution to our audience, and a permanent live link to a page we have reviewed for accuracy.

## What we publish

Our coverage sits in five areas. A strong pitch lands inside one of them.

| Area | We publish | A weak pitch sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| Web performance | Core Web Vitals work, load budgets, hosting and CDN tradeoffs | "10 ways to speed up your website" |
| AI integration | AI agents, answer engine optimization, LLM tooling in production | "AI will change everything, here is why" |
| Local SEO | Google Business Profile, citations, reviews, local landing pages | Generic keyword advice with no local specifics |
| Digital architecture | Full-stack builds, frameworks, deployment, web performance | Framework tutorials that copy the official docs |
| Technical deep-dives | Post-mortems, benchmarks, code-level explainers | Topical roundups with no original analysis |

A pitch needs numbers somewhere in it. A latency figure, a revenue number, a traffic change, a before-and-after screenshot, a cost you actually pay. Without specifics we cannot tell whether you have done the work or read about it, and that is the first thing we screen for.

## The three requirements

**1. Original work.** Write it for us, not for us. If the same article has already appeared on your own site, your LinkedIn, or a Medium republication, say so plainly in your pitch. We are not able to publish something already published elsewhere, and we would rather decline honestly than discover it later and have to run a correction.

**2. Demonstrate expertise.** Tell us what you have actually done. A live site you maintain, a repository, a role, a client project, a metric you are responsible for. Your [author page]({{ '/authors/' | relative_url }}) gets created after your first piece is accepted, so a link to something you have built matters more than a job title.

**3. Follow the house rules.** This is the part that gets submissions rejected fastest, so read it carefully.

## Links: what we allow and what we delete

We allow up to **two contextual internal links** to posts on this site, and up to **one external link** to a resource you genuinely built. Both are at our editorial discretion and neither is guaranteed.

Everything below gets deleted without discussion, and repeated violations end the submission:

- Paid placements, sponsored links, or any link bought through a network
- Exact-match anchor text chosen for ranking. "AI shopping agents" is fine as a phrase in a sentence, "best AI shopping agent software" as an anchor is not
- Reciprocal link arrangements, guest post exchanges, and link-for-traffic swaps
- Links in the author bio rather than in context, and links with no relevance to the sentence they sit in
- AI-generated or spun content, including AI text lightly reworded to pass detection

## Style we want

- **Short paragraphs.** One to three sentences. We break up dense blocks.
- **No em dashes or en dashes.** Use commas or full stops.
- **Plain, specific language.** Name the tool, the version, the number.
- **Direct answers early.** Say what something is and whether it works in the first two paragraphs, because AI assistants quote that part.
- **Reader-centered.** Write to the person who will act on this, not to a search engine.
- **Tables for comparisons, lists for sequences, prose for everything else.** If a section is five bullets with no verbs, it wants to be a paragraph.

## Length and format

1,200 to 2,000 words is the working range. We accept longer when the subject genuinely requires it, and we will cut aggressively if it does not. Longer posts get cut more often than short ones, so write the tight version first.

Send the draft as Markdown, Google Docs, or plain text. If your piece includes code, tables, or screenshots, tell us how you want them laid out before you spend time on formatting.

## How we review

1. **Pitch screen.** We read every pitch. If the topic fits and you can show the work, you get a reply. Expect about a week, longer if the queue is deep.
2. **Draft review.** Once you send the draft, an editor checks facts, links, and house style, and sends consolidated notes rather than a stream of emails.
3. **Verification.** Claims about platform behavior, algorithm changes, or benchmarks get checked against primary sources. If we cannot verify a figure, it does not run. We will ask you for the source.
4. **Final edit.** We tighten, cut, and restructure. We will not rewrite your argument into something you disagree with.
5. **Publication.** Your byline, author bio, and link go live together.

## How to submit

Email [{{ site.email }}](mailto:{{ site.email }}) with **"Guest pitch"** at the start of the subject line, so it does not get filed as a general inquiry.

Keep it under 300 words and include:

- **Working title** and the target keyword you intend to rank for
- **The angle in one sentence.** What do you know that the existing coverage on this topic does not?
- **Evidence you have done the work.** A link, a repository, a metric, a screenshot
- **A conflict disclosure.** Clients, competitors, products you sell, anything that would make a reader think the piece is an ad
- **Two or three relevant posts from this site** that your piece would sit next to

For a list of what we already cover, browse the [article archive]({{ '/archive.html' | relative_url }}). Pitches that ignore existing coverage on the site get declined, because we are not filling a gap that is already filled.

## What happens next

If we decline, you will get a reason. Most declines come down to one of four things: the topic is already covered here, the pitch shows no original work, the links do not pass the rules above, or we could not verify the central claim.

Declining a pitch is not a judgment about you. We publish a small number of pieces most weeks and receive many more.

## Before you write

Read the [editorial policy]({{ '/press/editorial-policy.html' | relative_url }}), which covers sourcing, review cadence, and our correction process. The [ethics and fact-checking policy]({{ '/press/ethics-policy.html' | relative_url }}) sets the standards for disclosure and conflicts. If you are unsure whether a topic is a fit, email first and ask. That costs you one email and saves you a draft.
