Some links on F9XR Articles are affiliate links. If you buy something through one, we may earn a commission. You pay the same price either way. This page explains exactly how that works, where the disclosures sit, and which links exist today.

## What an Affiliate Link Is

An affiliate link is a normal web link with a referral code attached. The code tells the vendor who referred the click.

Three things are true about every affiliate link on this site:

- **The price does not change.** You pay the vendor's normal price. Commissions come out of the vendor's marketing budget, not your bill.
- **Cookies expire.** Most programmes attribute a purchase for a limited window, typically 30 days. If you buy something on your own two months later, we earn nothing.
- **The recommendation stands on its own.** A commission is not a reason to recommend a product. It is also not a reason to hide one that is worse.

## How Commissions Are Earned

F9XR Articles runs an affiliate programme with the vendors whose tools we cover. When a reader clicks a link and later signs up or subscribes, the vendor reports that sale back to us and we receive a commission.

We do not get paid for:

- Reading the documentation.
- Forming an opinion about a tool.
- Deciding not to recommend something.
- Correcting a pricing page.

The commercial relationship exists. It does not reach the editorial desk. If a commission and a recommendation ever disagree, the recommendation wins, and we correct the article. That is written into the [Ethics & Fact-Checking Policy]({{ '/press/ethics-policy.html' | relative_url }}) as well.

## Where Disclosures Appear

An article that contains affiliate links carries a visible notice before any purchasing advice, not buried at the bottom. The notice names the vendor, states that a commission may be earned, and links back to this page.

| Placement | What it does |
|---|---|
| Top of the article | Names the vendor and flags the commission before the first recommendation |
| Pricing and comparison sections | Affiliate links are marked as such in the sentence that introduces them |
| Footer of every page | Links to this disclosure so any article is covered |

Vendor pricing, task limits, and feature lists are always sourced from the vendor's own pages. Where an article quotes a number, it says so, and it says to confirm on the official page before you pay. Third-party figures drift and we have no way to freeze them.

## Editorial Independence

Three commitments hold across every affiliate relationship:

- **Same standard of evidence.** A tool covered on an affiliate link is held to the same testing bar as one covered without.
- **No pay-for-placement.** We do not accept payment for a positive mention, a ranking position, or a favourable comparison.
- **Disagreement is published.** Where a product is a worse fit than the alternative, the article says so. The commission does not get a vote.

Full detail sits in the [Ownership, Funding, & Transparency]({{ '/press/transparency.html' | relative_url }}) page.
